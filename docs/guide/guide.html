DeusEdix: the guide

DeusEdix is a map editor for Deus Ex and Deus Ex: Revision. It opens the original Revision maps in 3D, with the game's real models and textures, and lets you change them without UnrealEd:

  • move, rotate, scale, copy and delete objects and whole structures (a helipad, a building);
  • add any object of the game, blocks (walls, platforms) and ready-made pieces of other levels;
  • replace the texture of an object or a structure with your own picture;
  • change the properties that matter for a mission (locked doors, keypad codes, computer passwords, what a crate contains, what a guard does and carries...);
  • write the mission's story: dialogues, InfoLinks, events and actions, compiled for you;
  • make your own characters from the faces, bodies and clothes of the game's characters;
  • make new maps (an empty room or a copy of any map) and link them to each other and to the game's maps.

The game's files are never modified: existing maps are edited on a copy of your own, and what you make goes into a folder of DeusEdix's (Revision\DeusEdixMaps).

DeusEdix is a fan-made tool, not affiliated with the Deus Ex: Revision team, Eidos or Square Enix.

Start it

You need Deus Ex: Game of the Year Edition, from Steam or GOG. Deus Ex: Revision (Steam) is optional: it goes into the same steamapps\common\Deus Ex folder and lets you make maps for Revision too. Each project is made for one of the two games (New project > Game). The installer finds the folder; if it cannot, DeusEdix asks for it when it starts (or run deusedix-cli.exe --set-game "<Deus Ex folder>" in DeusEdix's folder). With a Steam copy, Play starts the game through Steam; a GOG copy starts by itself.

Start DeusEdix from the Start menu. The first time it prepares its workspace from your game (links and copies inside its own folder: nothing of the game is changed) and compiles its package, which takes a few seconds. The 3D view needs an internet connection (it loads three.js, the 3D engine, from a CDN).

DeusEdix's window needs Windows 10 or 11 with Microsoft Edge WebView2 (part of Windows; Microsoft's WebView2 Runtime installs it if it is missing). Without it, or under Wine or Proton (Linux, Steam Deck), DeusEdix opens the editor in your web browser instead; DeusEdix.exe --browser does the same on purpose. It closes by itself when you close its last page.

The start screen lets you create a new map, open an existing map, or take Dr. Hale's tutorial.

The first time you open a map the editor analyses it and takes what the 3D view needs from the game (from a few seconds to a minute); after that it opens at once.

Opening and closing maps

The editor starts on New Map / Open Map, with a card for Dr. Hale's tutorial and your eight most recent maps. New Map opens the dialog of an empty room, a copy of a map or one of your map templates. Open Map searches your maps, recent maps and original levels grouped by mission. You can search by filename, location or mission name. The list in the top bar remains a quick switcher:

  • Script-built maps: maps made from scripts (tools\mapkit\maps\), including new empty rooms. Your changes are kept next to the script and applied on every rebuild.
  • Mod maps: your project's copies of existing maps (maps\).
  • Original maps (you edit a copy): every Revision map. Changes are saved separately and built on a copy.

The first time you open a map the editor analyses it (from a few seconds to a minute); after that it opens at once.

Browse for a .dx file... imports a map into the project's maps folder. Check the copy name and press Import copy. Existing names are refused: choose another name to keep both maps. The original file and the game's folders stay untouched. A map without a build is marked Not built yet; pending edits/builds are marked Changed, not installed.

File > Close map returns to the start screen. If the map or Story has unsaved changes, choose Save changes, Discard or Cancel. Saving here stores your work; Apply in game and Rebuild are still separate operations. Settings > General > When the editor opens chooses Start screen (the default) or Last map. Clear recent clears the list and last-map memory.

Interactive tutorial

Help > Interactive tutorial opens an eight-room training wing run by Dr. Elias Hale. The route turns through an L-shaped loading bay and a horseshoe drill course, with smaller rooms for conversation, materials and reading. Observation glass is used in reception and security. Show me frames the lesson and selects its object. The panel notices completed exercises:

  1. Reception: fly around the editor; meet Hale behind the original training observation window.
  2. Stores: select and move a crate, then place a new crate on top of the Crate stockpile platform around the corner. Pulsing circles guide you from the Objects search to the crate and the drop zone.
  3. Door systems: connect the wall button to either leaf of the double door; both leaves open together.
  4. Drill hall: give the trooper a greeting animation in the horseshoe practice course.
  5. Lab office: write a dialogue for the scientist.
  6. Materials workshop: select the plain crate, open Textures > Edit, paint its skin and choose On this object only > Apply. Hale's smiling demonstration crate is beside it. Merely saving a picture in My textures does not complete this exercise.
  7. Film stage: frame The Last Cup, a guarded mug of coffee. Tools > Add a cutscene camera (or + Cutscene) records the first view. Move the playhead to about four seconds, move the camera or change its framing, and press K for another key. Play the complete preview, then connect the SCREENING button to the camera with Play the cutscene. The exercise checks for two distinct keys in the stage, a preview of the current shot and a working button rule.
  8. Field test and library: Apply in game in Story, Rebuild, then Play from here. Test the door, greeting, dialogue, painted crate and screening button; finish with the library's tips.

The map is generated locally from the script in tools/mapkit/tutorial/ and the installed game's assets. Reception, observation booths and door leaves reuse selected architecture from the local 00_Training, preserving scale and texture alignment. Original map files are never modified or bundled. The burgundy reception floor uses the engine's mirror flag; the browser's Lit preview does not reproduce that reflection, so use View in game to inspect it.

The advanced wing is optional and opens north of the library, for those who want more than the basics. Every basic step offers Advanced ›, and Advanced wing sits next to Finish; the panel counts the training and the wing apart and never moves from one to the other by itself:

  • Interrogation: a dialogue with JC's answers (choices), a consequence that opens the vault, and a story flag the informant remembers next time.
  • Demolition range: a timer that counts down, what happens when it runs out, and a keypad that defuses the bomb.
  • Ambush yard: a trigger zone that sends two attackers at JC.
  • Code lab: your first script (Tools > Scripts), wired to a button with an interaction, then tried live with the Console while the game runs.

Dr. Hale speaks his lines (his voice is in the editor and in the game). The speaker button in the head of his panel (Mute tutorial audio) silences his voice and the background music.

Exercise edits and custom dialogue are kept when the tutorial's stock text is updated. Start over on the last step clears the training edits and story and rebuilds the map. The first time the editor opens, Hale offers the course; it remains available in Help.

An Alt click without a drag still picks a face (see Faces and texture alignment), and Alt dragging a gizmo handle still copies the object. After F or an orbit the camera keeps turning round the same point while it looks at it, even with nothing selected.

The axis widget in the top right corner of the 3D view shows the view's axes as the gizmo does (X red, Y green and up, Z blue: the Inspector's axes). Click an end to look from that side toward the middle of the view: Y looks down from above, with X to the right as in the Top view; X and Z look level. Shift+click opens the matching Top, Front or Side view instead. Under it, where the camera faces in the game's units: the letter of the game's compass (North is the map's TrueNorth) and the yaw (65536 a turn).

Settings > Camera: the flying speed (also the wheel while flying), the mouse sensitivity, invert, the field of view and clipping. The field of view is the one of your game (DefaultFOV in RevisionUser.ini, User.ini for the original game; else the game's own: 86° in Revision, 75° in the original), so the view frames what the player sees; move the slider for another one, Game's goes back to it. A new cutscene camera starts with it too. Near and Far set how close and how far the view draws.

Lighting of the view

Like Lit and Unlit in Unity or Unreal, the list next to Help (or the L key) changes how the 3D view is lit:

Shadedthe editor's own soft light: everything is easy to see; good for building
Litthe game's lighting: the light and the shadows of every lamp, their colour, how far they reach, spotlights, the ambient light of each zone. What you see is close to what the game shows
Unlitthe textures only, without any light

In Lit:

  • Walls, floors and ceilings show the map's real lightmaps, with their shadows.
  • Objects and characters are lit vertex by vertex like the engine does: the side facing a lamp is bright, the other side dark. A lamp lights an object only if the object's centre can see it.
  • Neon signs, screens and other unlit surfaces stay at full brightness, as in the game.
  • A slider appears next to the list: it makes the whole view brighter or darker, to compare with your screen. It does not change the game.

Lit shows the lighting of the last rebuild: lamps you add, move or change are not shown until you press Rebuild. An object you add or move takes the lamps around its new place at once, but without their shadows until the rebuild.

UnrealEd's views: the same list (and View in the menu bar) also has UnrealEd's Wireframe (the outline of every surface, the textures hidden; the objects stay as they are), BSP cuts (the textured world with the edges of every BSP polygon on it: where the engine split the surfaces when it built the map) and Zones (every surface in the colour of its zone, with a legend: the rooms that zone portals and ZoneInfo actors make). They show the last rebuild; a map read by an older version of the editor shows its zones after one Rebuild. Choosing a lighting brings the textured world back.

Lightmap detail (in the same list, as Unity's lightmap resolution view): every wall, floor and ceiling in the colour of how far apart the samples of its lightmap are, the points the engine lights it on (its shadows are as sharp as that), with a check for each sample. The legend gives the colours (up to 12 units a sample: very sharp and the most memory; 24 to 48: the usual, about 32; over 96: very soft) and the map's lightmaps: how many, how many samples, and about how much memory they take as the game's 32-bit lightmaps (an estimate: the map file keeps a bit a sample for each lamp instead). Grey is a surface without a lightmap (unlit, sky, invisible). It shows the last Rebuild: change a face's Shadow detail (see Shadow detail), Rebuild, and look again. A face only a few samples big gets an estimate of its size.

Lights here: Ctrl+Alt+click a wall, a floor or a ceiling (in any lighting; Alt+click alone still picks the face). A small menu lists the lamps that light that point, brightest first, with their share of the light there, then the lamps whose reach gets there but that a wall hides; click one to select it. It is an estimate made in the editor from the lamps as they are now (their radius, brightness, colour, the angle they meet the surface at and a spotlight's cone, and a ray from the point to each lamp through the walls of the 3D view), not from the lightmaps: the game's shadows come from the last Rebuild, and objects do not hide a lamp. A lamp the project's game never lights (a flickering lamp in Revision, or there a palette lamp whose Skin is Effects.Corona.Corona_G) is not listed, and Light preview (in the same list as Lit: the lamps as they are now, without shadows) leaves it dark too.

Effects (the sun button next to the list, or View > Effects), as Unity's Scene view Effects: in the Lit view the map moves as the game does. It is kept for the next time (in the browser). While it is on:

  • Lamps whose Type is pulse, subtle pulse, blink, flicker or strobe change as the game's renderer makes them, in the game the project is for (Revision and the original game differ): a pulse goes from 21 to 99% of the lamp (60% on average) and a subtle pulse from 81 to 99%, a round every Period / 35 seconds (32: about 0.9 s), Phase / 256 of a round later. A blink keeps no time: it is on or off at random, in about half the frames, whatever its Period, and its Phase does nothing. It stays on for good after a while: in Revision only when Period + 1 is a power of two (Period 0: after 256 s, 1: after 512 s, 3, 7, 15...: later; the default 32 never does), in the original game whatever the Period, after about 7.3 x (Period + 1) seconds (32: about 4 minutes); the game counts from the start of the map, the editor from when Effects starts showing. A flicker is dark in Revision: its renderer never lights one (the original maps' flickering lamps are dark too: use a blink); in the original game it is off in about half the frames, else at 50 to 100% of the lamp, a new value every frame. Every strobe of the map switches together, on one frame and off the next, whatever its Period and Phase. Palette types (texture palette once / loop) and the light effects (torch, searchlight...) stay steady, but in Revision a palette lamp whose Skin is Effects.Corona.Corona_G is dark (measured: that Skin gives it no light).
  • On objects and characters each lamp's own share of their light goes up and down: as the Lit view lights them. On walls, floors and ceilings the lightmaps hold the light of every lamp together, so the editor takes a lamp's share as its light at that point without shadows, never more than the lightmap holds there: behind a wall within its reach the light dims a little too. The 16 moving lamps nearest the camera move on the walls; the others stay steady there. The lamps are those of the last Rebuild, as their Type, Period and Phase are now (a value set in All properties first: it wins at Rebuild).
  • Lamps with Corona on and a Skin (the texture the game draws as the corona: set it in All properties; the original maps use Effects.Corona.Corona_E, F or G) show a glow of their colour where they are, the same size on the screen at any distance, as the game draws it: in a square a fifth of the view's width at DrawScale 1, its bright middle about a tenth of that square across, fading out within the middle half of it (as Revision's Corona_G does; the original game's textures were not measured). It shows at full strength whatever the lamp does (a dark flickering lamp's corona still shines, and so does a lamp of brightness 0: the original maps' glows); at most 32, the nearest; none for a lamp whose Enabled in game is unticked (it is out of the map). A wall coming between it and the camera fades it out in a third of a second, and back in. Without a Skin the game draws no corona, and neither does the editor.
  • Surfaces with Auto U Pan or Auto V Pan move their texture at their zone's speed (TexUPanSpeed, TexVPanSpeed: 35 texels a second at 1), water, waterfalls and clouds. Select the zone actor and open Inspector > Texture panning to set U speed and V speed: 0 stops, 1 is normal, 2 is twice as fast and -1 reverses it. These speeds affect every surface with the corresponding Auto Pan flag in that zone, including sky zones. If there is no zone actor, add Zone Info from Objects > Zones inside the space first. Rebuild applies the speeds in the game and updates their View > Effects preview.
  • With the camera in a zone that has a view fog (ViewFog, ViewFlash: water, toxic liquid), the whole view takes it as JC's view does with his head in that zone; a zone with a FogDistance and a FogColor fades to that colour with distance. The game's volumetric fog (lamps' fog brightness) is not drawn.

The zones' speeds and fog are those of the last Rebuild; a map read by an older version of the editor pans at the usual speed and shows no zone fog until it is opened again. Effects costs nothing while it is off: the view is drawn every frame only while Effects shows.

The screen

The layout follows the familiar hierarchy, scene, inspector and asset browser arrangement:

  • Menus and toolbar: File, Edit, View, Map, Tools, Window, Settings and Help. The Windows app uses a native menu above the editor; the browser keeps these menus in the top bar. Save, Rebuild and Play remain beside the current map. Themes and Rounded variants are in Settings. Edit > Search everything… (Ctrl+K) finds any command, object of the map, object to place, texture or sound by its name (see Searching everything).
  • Hierarchy (left): every object of the level. The structures (helipad, buildings...) with their objects inside, then the other objects by kind, as the 3D view's Show menu sorts them: characters, lights, objects, doors, triggers, AI paths, blocks (with the walls, roads, floors, ceilings, stairs and roofs you drew), sounds, cameras and cutscenes, zones. Click a line to select it, double-click it (or press F) to go to it, the bin deletes it. The search box finds any object by name or kind, and by its Tag, Event, sounds, textures, model or state, or what points at it (see Searching the map). Bulbs identify lights, people identify NPCs, and cubes, doors and routes identify objects, movers and AI paths. NPC icons show their configured relation towards JC at map start: green for allies, grey for neutral characters and red for enemies. Class defaults and unsaved relation changes are included, and Undo updates the colour too. Hover over an icon for its meaning. Player starts have a flag and helicopters have their own icon. A single piece taken out of a structure has the piece icon, not the structure's.
  • Browser (bottom), the media library, its sections on the left. Game: Objects (every object of the game: your own characters first, then the most used, the ways in and out of a map, decorations, weapons, characters...), Models (every 3D model), Pieces (every object of the original levels made of brushes, whole: see Adding things), Sounds. Prefabs: the structures and nests you saved, reusable in every level of the project, their copies linked to them (see Adding things). Imported: My models and My sounds, what you brought in with Import... (or dropped on the editor). The Console tab next to it shows the running game's log (see Scripts and the Console). Click a tile, then a floor or a wall to place it, or drag it onto the 3D view; sounds are dragged (see Sounds). + Create adds a block, a hole, water, a zone portal, a sign or a cutscene camera; Characters... opens the Character Creator. Nothing assembled by hand is in the library: the structures of the map you are editing are in the Hierarchy. Favourites, as in Unity's Project window: the star of a tile (it shows under the pointer) keeps that object, character, model, piece, mover, prefab or sound in a Favourites group at the top of its section, for this project (textures have theirs in the texture picker, see Textures); its star again takes it out. A favourite whose asset is gone (a mod's package removed, a prefab or a sound of yours deleted) stays there, dashed and Missing, until its star takes it out. The fixed Most used list of Objects stays as it was. The slider at the top right of the Browser makes the tiles a list (one line each, with the class or the model's own name after the name) or small, medium or large tiles; Ctrl+wheel over the tiles does the same, and then neither the page nor the 3D view zooms. One size for every section, kept in this browser.
  • Inspector (right): the selected object's transform and relevant properties in foldouts. Changes and Messages can be expanded when needed. Right-click an object for actions for its type: a character offers Dialogues and Animation Studio; a light offers Light settings. The padlock at the top keeps the Inspector on this object while you select others (to drag something onto its fields); press it again to follow the selection. Right click > Properties… (in the 3D view or the Hierarchy) opens a floating Inspector of that one object instead, while the panel keeps following the selection (see Properties and connections).
  • History (a tab next to the Inspector; Window > History or Ctrl+U), as Unity's Undo History: every change of this map by its name (Move 3 objects, Light brightness of Light4, Apply texture Wall_A, Fill AI paths), the newest at the top and Map opened at the bottom. The step the map is at is marked, the steps Redo would bring back are dimmed. Click a step to go back (or forward) to it: the map is drawn once, however many steps that takes, and the status bar says how many; Up / Down move in the list, Enter goes (the focus stays on the step gone to). Edit > Undo and Redo say the step they take (Undo Move 3 objects), and after Ctrl+Z the status bar says what was undone. A click that changes nothing (on the gizmo, an animation key or a key of the cutscene timeline, let go where it was) is no step, and the steps Redo would bring back stay. Ctrl+U brings the panel to the front, or hides it when it is there already (while a panel or the 3D view is maximized over it, Ctrl+U shows it and the layout comes back). The last 100 steps are kept (past that the bottom row says Before the oldest step kept). The history belongs to the map open: opening another map, a Rebuild (it loads the map again) or reloading the page starts it again, empty. Your changes are not lost with it: Save and autosave (Settings > General, every 5 minutes at first) keep them. While a character is posed with Pose here, Ctrl+Z is the pose's own and the History panel waits.
  • The 3D view's toolbar (top left of the view, like Unity's): Move, Rotate, Scale (W E R), Center / Pivot (Z) and Global / Local (X) for the gizmo (see Objects), the arrows of the previous and next selection, grid snap (G) with its grid step and rotation step, the grid shown in the view at the floor of the selection, the bulb that shows or hides the icons of lights, AI paths and logic points (see Objects), and the Show menu (the stacked layers: each kind of thing shown, locked and with its icons, see Visibility, selection and game activation). Then Isolate (Shift+H) and the crossed eye with the number of objects hidden with the eye (see Visibility, selection and game activation), and the camera that pilots the selection (Shift+P, see The selection and the view under Objects); while one is on, a flag says so (Isolated: 3 objects, Hidden shown: 5, Piloting Light) with a button that ends it. Last, the view's lighting with Effects (see Lighting of the view) and the speaker that plays the map's ambient sounds round the camera (see Hear the map under Sounds).
  • The axis widget (top right of the view, like Unity's scene gizmo): click an axis end to look from that side, with the camera's heading under it (see Moving around).
  • Panels: drag a panel tab to the left, right or bottom edge, or into the scene to float it. The panel menu also offers docking and hiding. Resize by dragging its edge. Window shows hidden panels and offers Default, Scene and Inspect layouts, plus Reset layout. The layout is remembered on this computer. Floating panels remain inside the editor window. Saved layouts, as Unity's Layout menu: Window > Save layout as… keeps the panels as they are (where each one is docked, how wide, which tab is in front) under a name of yours, for writing the Story, a lighting pass, texture work or play-testing with the Console. Each one is then an item of the Window menu (Layout: and its name) that puts them back at once; a name used again replaces it. Up to 10, kept in this browser for every project (they are yours, not the project's). Delete layout… lists them with a Delete button each; Export layouts… writes them all to deusedix-layouts.json, Import layouts… adds those of such a file (on another computer, or after clearing the browser), one with a name you have already replacing yours. Maximize panel (Shift+Space, Window menu), as Unity's: the docked panel under the pointer fills the editor, with its tabs; over the 3D view, the 3D view fills it (floating panels stay). Shift+Space again, or Esc, puts the layout back; so does any change of the panels (docking, hiding, a layout from the menu). The layout you had is never changed by it, nor stored maximized. Over a floating panel or a window nothing happens (the status bar says so). The key can be changed in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts.

Visibility, selection and game activation

  • The eye hides an object only in the scene view. Its game behaviour is unchanged.
  • The lock prevents accidental selection, snapping onto it and edits in the scene. You can still select its Hierarchy row to inspect it or unlock it. View > Show all objects and Unlock all objects recover these editor-only states, which are remembered per map on this computer.
  • Show (the stacked layers in the 3D view's toolbar, as Unity's Layers and Gizmos menus): a row for each kind of thing, as the Hierarchy sorts them (Structures, Characters, Lights, Objects, Doors and elevators, Triggers and logic, AI paths, Blocks with the drawings, then Sounds, Cameras and cutscenes and Zones wherever they are: a class named for a sound or music, a camera or a cutscene, or a zone, the game's or a mod's; a character stays a character whatever its name, as the engine's Camera), with how many the map has. Its eye hides the whole kind in the 3D view (hidden blocks are their boxes: what the last Rebuild built of them is the map's own and stays, as with Isolate); its padlock locks it: not picked by a click or a rectangle, left out of Select all, not snapped to (V, Ctrl+Shift), no gizmo or dots, its settings greyed in the Inspector (Drop to floor and placing from the browser still land on it); its bulb (the kinds drawn as icons) hides only that kind's icons. A click on a kind's name shows only that kind (to follow the triggers and logic of a room), again shows every kind; Show, unlock and draw every kind puts them all back. Icon size makes every icon of the view bigger or smaller. Esc closes the menu (and the toolbar's other lists) and does nothing else: the next Esc ends a placing or clears the selection. The Hierarchy's kind folders are these same rows (so a sound sits in Sounds, not in Triggers and logic) and have the same eye and padlock, which cover everything the folder lists (the Structures folder's too; Blocks lists the drawings, each with its own eye and padlock); a row of a hidden kind is greyed, of a locked one in italics, and says why when you hover it. A structure, or a folder of yours, still moves and deletes as one with a hidden or locked kind inside it: selected whole or with other things, dragged in the Hierarchy, moved into another folder or taking things moved into it, and edited from its right-click menu; only the own eye or padlock of something in it holds it. What you place, paste, draw, duplicate or make in any other way (the Hierarchy's +, the 2D Shape Editor, Clip, a hole's light, Fill AI paths, a patrol route's points, the sky's suns, a cutscene camera, Replace with) of a hidden or locked kind is made all the same, and the status bar says that its kind is hidden or locked. These states are kept per project on this computer, apart from each object's own eye and padlock: an object shows only when both let it, and showing a kind again never turns an object's own eye back on (nor View > Show all objects a kind's). The toolbar's button is lit while a kind is off.
  • Isolate (Shift+H, the frame button of the 3D view's toolbar, or View > Isolate selection), as Unity's: only the selection stays in the view, with what it holds (the objects of a structure, what a folder holds) and the structure each selected object stands in, so a desk is seen in its room. Everything else is hidden until you press Shift+H again or Exit on the toolbar's flag; opening another map or starting Play ends it too. Objects you add meanwhile (a copy, a paste) are shown. The map, the eyes and Save are untouched; in the Hierarchy the rows outside the isolation are greyed. The map's own surfaces that are in no structure (the built blocks) stay.
  • Show hidden (the crossed eye of the toolbar, with how many objects the eye hides, or View > Show hidden objects for now) shows them for a while without turning their eye back on: press it again (or Hide on its flag) and they are hidden as before. Inside an isolation the eye still hides, and Show hidden reveals only what is isolated. View > Show all objects, instead, turns every eye back on.
  • Enabled in game controls whether an actor exists in the next playable Rebuild. An unchecked object remains in the editor with its properties, animation and dialogue configuration intact. Toggle it back on and rebuild to restore it. This change supports Undo and Save; structural BSP groups use the eye and lock, without an actor activation switch.

Authored interactions involving inactive actors are skipped with a build message. Original mission scripts may still expect their actors, so test the mission after disabling one. For existing .dx maps, the editable source is retained in maps/.authoring/ with its edits; keep that folder with the project when moving it. Build caches can be reconstructed from the retained source.

The viewport uses a coloured bulb for lights, a camera for cutscene cameras, a walking figure for AI path points, a speaker for sounds and distinct symbols for triggers and other logic objects.

Searching the map

The Hierarchy's search box works as Unity's scene search. Words find a name, the label the Hierarchy shows or a class, as before (crate, patrol point); with several words, each one must be there. Tokens look at one thing of an object:

t: (or class:)its class, the classes it comes from, or its kind: t:light, t:door, t:character, t:sound, t:camera, t:zone, t:structure, t:ScriptedPawn
tag: / event:its Tag / its Event: tag:alarm finds what an alarm's Event sets off, event:alarm what sets it off
name: / label:its name in the map (name:DeusExMover3) / the name the Hierarchy shows
snd:a sound it uses: an ambient sound, a door's or an elevator's sounds, a sound effect's, a sound its Interactions rules play (the sounds set on it in the map or here; a class's own default sounds are not known)
tex:a texture it uses: its skins, the faces of a structure, a block's, one put on it in the Textures section
mesh:its model: mesh:Chair, or a model's package, mesh:DeusExDeco
is:is:hidden, is:locked (by its padlock or its kind's), is:disabled (in game), is:added (in the editor), is:original (of the map), is:nested (in a folder, or inside a structure), is:selected
ref:ref:Button1: that object, what points at it and what it points at (Event to Tag, the keys of a door, the patrols, the Inspector's Connections) and its Interactions rules both ways

tag:alarm finds a part of the value; tag=Alarm the whole of it (for a sound, a texture or a model also its last part: snd=Hum for Ambient.Ambient.Hum). A value with spaces goes in quotes (tag:"Alarm Lights"). A - before a word or a token leaves out what it finds (t:light -is:hidden), wherever it is. A folder or a structure whose name has a word you look for shows all it holds (the folders and structures in it too, with what they hold), but what a - word or token leaves out; with - words alone, a folder or a structure they leave empty is not listed. Words and tokens can be mixed, and every one must hold: t:door snd:metal is:original. While you type, the tokens are offered under the box as small chips, then the values this map has for one (its tags, its classes, its sounds...): Up / Down choose one, Tab or Enter (or a click) puts it in. Esc in the box clears the search (pressed again, the keys go back to the 3D view).

While a search is on, the 3D view dims everything that does not match: the matches stay bright, and their parts hidden behind walls glow through them, so you see at once where the alarm triggers or the doors of a sound are (the Top, Front and Side views stay as they are). The gizmo and the selection's outline stay bright. Clearing the box brings the view back as it was; the objects themselves and their materials are not changed, and nothing of the search is saved. Opening another map (or closing this one) ends the search: a name of one map means nothing, or another object, in the next.

Find references (right click an object or a moving block, or Edit > Find references), as Unity's Find References in Scene: the search becomes ref: and its name. Find in map in the browsers asks which objects use an asset: in a sound's Inspector (Sounds), and by right click on a model (Models) or on a texture's picture (the texture picker): the search becomes snd=, mesh= or tex= and the name.

Searching everything

Edit > Search everything… (Ctrl+K), as Unity's Search window: one box over the whole editor. Type part of a name: the letters of each word are looked for in their order, best at the start of a word, so rbl finds Rebuild lighting and keypad finds both the keypads of this map and the keypad to place. The results come in groups, the group with the best match first:

  • Commands: every command of the menus, with its menu and its key. What its menu greys out now (a map command with no map open, Paste properties with nothing copied) is greyed here too and does not run; the rest runs exactly as from its menu. The Window menu's panels are under Panels instead.
  • In this map: the map's objects, found as the Hierarchy's search finds them; Enter selects one and frames it, as a double click in the Hierarchy.
  • Place an object: the items of the Objects browser, your characters too; Enter starts placing it (click a floor or a wall).
  • Textures (this map's first, then the game's) and Sounds (the game's and yours): Enter opens it in its browser, the texture picker on its picture (right click: Find in map) or the Sounds browser with that sound chosen (its search becomes the sound's name).
  • Settings (each page, found also by what is on it: fov finds Camera) and Panels (Hierarchy, Inspector, Asset browser, Console and the browser's sections): Enter opens it (a panel comes to the front, never hides).

> at the start looks only at the commands (> alone lists them all), @ only at this map's objects (@ alone lists them all, and the Hierarchy's tokens are offered as you type). A query with a token of the Hierarchy's search (t:light, tag:alarm, is:hidden: see Searching the map) searches the map's objects too. Up / Down choose, Enter runs (a click too), Tab puts in the token offered, Esc closes. With the box empty, your last 20 searches come back (kept per project on this computer). The box does not open over another window (Settings, the Story, the Animation Studio...), while the game is played, or while you type in a field, where Ctrl+K stays the field's. Its key can be changed in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts.

Maths in the number fields, as in Unity: the numbers of the Transform (position, rotation, pitch, roll, scale, a block's size) and of the Properties (a light's brightness and radius, a door's strength, Movement, the numbers of All properties) take a sum, worked out when you press Enter or leave the field: 2+3, 10/4, (64+16)/2, 2^3, 7%4, pi, sqrt(2), sin, cos, tan, floor, ceil, round (angles in radians: pi is half a turn). +=16, -=8, *=0.8 and /=2 work on the value the field had: +=16 in Y raises a crate by 16. With several objects selected, L(a,b) and R(a,b) spread a value over them, evenly or at random (see Editing several objects at once below); in a field of one object they are refused.

Rotate (E) turns an object every way, as in Unity: the green ring turns it round, the red and blue rings tip it forward or onto its side (a book lying on a lectern, a tilted sign), the outer ring turns it as seen from the camera. The Inspector shows Rotation, Pitch and Roll; type them for exact values. Pieces, structures, blocks, characters, doors and groups all support three-axis rotation. Selection outlines follow the object's orientation rather than expanding along the map's axes. A block keeps its size when it turns, also with grid snap on. An NPC's AI may turn it again while playing; editor placement does not disable its behaviour.

Aim at a point (Unity's look-at): the selected objects turn their front to a point of the 3D view, each from its own place: a security camera at a door, a spotlight at a painting, a turret down a corridor, a guard toward the entrance. Point at a wall, a floor or an object and press T; or, with Rotate (E), Ctrl+Shift+click the point (while Ctrl+Shift are held a dashed line goes from each object to the pointer); or right click > Aim at a point… (also in the Edit menu), then click the point (Esc cancels). Lights, cameras, turrets and effect points tip up or down too; characters, the player start, AI path points, furniture and items turn round only and stay standing (their pitch stays). Roll always stays. Several objects each aim at the same point; blocks and structures have no front and keep their turn. One Ctrl+Z undoes it.

Replace with (Unity's prefab Replace): the selected objects become another object, a character of yours or a model, each where it was, turned its way and at its size, under its own name. Twelve MJ12 troopers become UNATCO troopers, crates become barrels, a pickup another pickup. Click the replacement in the browser (Objects, your characters, or a model in Models: it waits to be placed), then right click the selection > Replace with… (or Edit > Replace with…). With nothing picked, the command waits for the next item you click in the browser (a row at the top of Objects says so; Cancel or Esc stops it). Or hold Alt while you let go of an item of the browser on an object: it replaces that object, or the whole selection when the object is part of it (with nothing under the pointer it is placed as usual).

  • What goes over: the place (an object or a character stands with its feet where the old object's were; a light, a logic point and something tipped, a lamp on a wall, keep their middle), the turn (a character only turns round), the size (the map's own DrawScale and the editor's scale), Tag, Event, the conversation name, a character's orders and patrol route, and every other property of the old object the new class also has, checked against the class's full list (as All properties shows it), values of All properties too. The look (model, skins, collision) is the new class's, or the model's or character's you picked.
  • Because the name stays, the Interactions that use the object, the patrol routes, the cutscenes that look at it, the conditions and the Story (dialogues, events) still find it, and so do its animation, its folder, and its hidden or disabled state. A conversation name the game's own conversations or a dialogue of the Story use stays on it even when the new class has another.
  • Before anything changes, a list shows what does not fit: the properties the new class has no place for, and the uses that no longer make sense, by what the new class offers to the rules (the catalogue of outputs and inputs): a rule when they are killed on a barrel, an action attack on a crate, a dialogue on something that cannot talk, a patrol route through what is no longer a patrol point. OK replaces anyway (those uses stay, to change or delete them later; the log lists them again), Cancel leaves everything as it was. With nothing to say, it just replaces.
  • One Ctrl+Z undoes the whole replacement. An object of the map becomes an object added in the editor: at Rebuild the map's own is removed and the new one takes its name (Delete of the replacement removes the map's own too).
  • Doors and lifts, blocks and structures keep their kind (pieces, blocks and movers of the browser cannot replace objects). Of an object of the map, only the properties the editor reads (its Properties panel, All properties set in the editor) go over; a replaced object inside a structure no longer moves with it (moving the structure later, or taking it apart, is fine: the map's own is still found and removed at Rebuild). Should the map's own not be found where it was (the map's script changed it), it stays and the new one is left out, and the build log says so: two objects never share a name.

The selection and the view (Unity's Align With View, Move To View, Align View to Selected and first person control of a camera). Fly to where a security camera, a spotlight, a turret, the player start or a guard should be and look the way it should look, then:

  • Align with view (Ctrl+Shift+F, Edit menu) puts the selection where the view looks from, turned the way it looks. Lights, cameras, turrets and effect points take the view's pitch too (and a roll of 0: the view has none); characters, the player start, furniture and items turn round only and stay standing, as with Aim at a point. A character goes with its eyes at the camera (its BaseEyeHeight, else 0.8 of its collision height, as the game does), so it sees what you saw; press End to drop it to the floor.
  • Move to view (Ctrl+Alt+F, Edit menu) moves the selection onto what the middle of the view looks at, a floor, a wall or an object, resting on it as Drop to floor does (on the grid along it with grid snap on), its turn kept. With nothing within 4000 units it goes in the air 400 units ahead.
  • Align view to selected (Shift+F, View menu) does the opposite: the view goes to the object and looks along its front, from a character's eyes. Select a security camera and press it to see what it watches.
  • Pilot selection (Shift+P, View menu, or the camera button of the 3D view's toolbar): the view looks through the object and flying the view (right mouse + W A S D, the wheel, panning) takes it along, turned as you look. A flag Piloting ... says so, the gizmo is off and the object itself is not drawn (the view is inside it). Shift+P again, Esc (also while you fly) or the flag's Exit ends it: the object stays where the view took it and the view goes back to where it was. Selecting something else, or clicking an empty place, ends it too.

With several objects selected they go as one: the last object you picked that has a front (a light, a camera, a character...) goes to the view, and the rest keep their place about it, turned with it round the vertical. Blocks and structures move with the group but cannot lead it (they have no front). Each command is one Ctrl+Z (none when the selection is already there); the moves of the view itself are not undone. The keys can be changed in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts.

Center / Pivot and Global / Local (the two buttons after Scale in the 3D view's toolbar, as Unity's; Z and X, or Edit > Gizmo: Pivot and Gizmo: Local). Center: a group's gizmo sits in the middle of the bounds of what is selected, and turns and scales it about there. Pivot: it sits on the last object you picked (pressing Z names it), and the group turns about that one: pick the chairs, then their table last, and they turn round it. A single object always turns about its own pivot. Global: the handles point along the map's axes. Local: they follow the object's own turn, its pitch and roll too (an object stuck to a slope, a tilted block, a ramp), and for a group the last object picked's; with grid snap on, a move then goes in whole grid steps along those axes from where the object was. The editor remembers both choices on this computer (Settings).

Scale (R): drag the middle square to scale evenly, as in Unity: right or up makes it bigger, left or down smaller, 1% for every pixel the mouse moves (5% steps with grid snap). It never flips inside out, however far you drag. The coloured handles scale one axis (blocks and structures).

Drag handles, as Unity's handles of lights, audio sources and colliders: a selected object shows dots on what it reaches, drawn over everything so that they can be grabbed through a wall; the dot under the pointer turns orange. Drag one: the value shows at the bottom of the view and in the status bar as you go (as its field in the Inspector will show it); let go and it is written in the units the game keeps it, one Ctrl+Z. Esc while dragging puts it back; then, as after a drag that ends where it began, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y are as they were. With grid snap on, sizes go in grid steps.

  • A light: six dots on its sphere set its Radius (LightRadius). The game's light reaches 25 × (radius + 1) units, so the reach goes in whole steps of 25 units, grid or not. A spotlight (Effect LE_Spotlight or LE_StaticSpot) shows its cone instead of the sphere, along its front (aim it with E or T): the dot at the tip sets its reach, the four on the rim how wide it opens (Cone, LightCone: 128 is 45° from the middle, 90° wide), in rotation steps with grid snap on.
  • A sound: the dots on its circles set Heard within: SoundRadius (steps of 25 units, as a light's radius) or a sound effect's own distance in units (FXRadius).
  • A trigger, or a sound effect played when JC walks in: four dots round its cylinder set its Radius (CollisionRadius), the top and bottom ones its Height (CollisionHeight, half the cylinder's height). The trigger stays in the middle.
  • A collision box: a dot in the middle of each side (its cylinder shape has the trigger's dots). Dragging one moves that side alone: the box's middle follows, so the opposite side stays where it is.
  • A block, with Scale (R): a dot in the middle of each side, also on holes, water and zone portals. Dragging one moves that side alone and the opposite side stays, as Unity's box handles: extend a floor to the next room, a wall up to the ceiling, a doorway to one jamb without moving the other. With grid snap on, a side facing along the map's axes lands on the grid (no slivers in the game's CSG); a turned block gets a whole number of grid steps thick. A side never crosses the opposite one (4 units at least). A hole's cut follows the drag. The coloured handles of the gizmo still scale from the middle.
  • Alt while dragging a side (a block or a collision box): both sides move, the middle stays. Shift: the other sides grow or shrink by the same share.

The dots work in the Top, Front and Side views too, each view with its own: a dot whose line points at you is left out of that view (it could not be dragged there) and shows in the others, so the top and bottom of a block that the Top view leaves out are dragged in the Front or Side view; in the 3D view, turn the view a little. Sides facing away from you are dimmer.

Grid snap (G, the magnet in the 3D view's toolbar, or Edit > Grid snap, ticked while it is on): moves go in steps of the grid step (units: a door is 64 wide), turns in steps of the rotation step (degrees). Type any number in the two boxes, or pick one with their arrow; they are the same as Settings > Editing. In Local, a move goes that many units from where it began, along the object's own axes. As in Unity, Ctrl held while dragging the gizmo turns grid snap the other way for as long as it is held (a free move with snap on, a snapped one with it off; a block's size too), and Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+] halve or double the grid step (Edit > Grid step: half and Grid step: double).

The grid in the 3D view: the grid button after the rotation step (or View > Grid in the 3D view) shows the grid on the floor, as Unity's scene grid: a cell for each grid step and a brighter line every 8 cells, fading out round the camera (from high up, every second or fourth line shows, so that it stays readable). It lies at the bottom of the selection, on the grid, and follows it while you move it; with nothing selected, on the floor under the camera. Walls and floors in front of it hide it; the Top, Front and Side views keep their own grid. The editor remembers whether it is shown.

Push to grid (Edit menu, Ctrl+\, Unity's Push To Grid): each selected thing goes onto the nearest grid point, for pieces pasted from UnrealEd and objects of the original maps that are a few units off. A block that is not turned goes by its lowest corner, so a block a whole number of grid steps long gets every side on the grid (no slivers in the game's CSG); a turned block by its middle; structures, Pieces and drawings by their pivot. An object keeps its height (it stands on its floor by its collision); only its X and Y go onto the grid. One Ctrl+Z undoes it; when everything is on the grid already, the status bar says so and nothing changes.

Snapping to corners and surfaces, as Unity's vertex and surface snapping, while you move something with the gizmo (W), one object or several:

  • Hold V while dragging: the corner of the selection nearest the pointer goes onto the corner nearest the pointer of the map, a structure, a block, a model or a drawing under it, when one is within a few pixels. A dot marks the corner that snaps: white while it looks for a corner, orange once it is on one. Away from any corner the move goes on as the gizmo makes it (on the grid with grid snap). The selection's corners are a block's own corners, the vertices of a structure, a Piece or a drawing, and for a model the corners of its bounds and its pivot (a light or another icon: its pivot); the corners it lands on are those of the faces under the pointer. It joins blocks to the off-grid pieces of the original maps without a gap. A tap of V outside a drag is still Edit vertices (the key of Edit vertices in Settings does both).
  • Hold Ctrl+Shift while dragging the middle square: the selection slides over the surface under the pointer (the map, a structure, a block, a model, a drawing; not itself) and rests on it by its bounds, as Drop to floor puts it on a floor: on a desk, on a shelf, against a wall. With grid snap it goes in grid steps along the surface. It keeps its turn; in Stick mode (the Drop button's menu) one object also turns to the surface, as Stick to surface turns it, and a decoration stuck to a wall or a slope gets Physics None so that it stays there in the game. Let go of Ctrl+Shift and it follows the gizmo again, with its own turn.

Each drag stays one Ctrl+Z. Turning and scaling are not snapped to corners or surfaces.

Icons: the bulb in the 3D view's toolbar hides the symbols of lights, AI path points and logic points, to see the level as it is; the Show menu next to it hides the icons of one kind (the sounds, the AI paths...) and sets the Icon size. Hidden icons cannot be clicked or taken by a rectangle in the view: pick them in the Hierarchy.

Objects scale all together (in Deus Ex an object has a single size, and its collision follows). Objects whose name starts with UC are the editor's own (interactions, the map's UCBoot...): you can move them, but keep them in the same room.

Particles: smoke, steam, sparks, dust

Objects > Effects has the game's particles, ready to place: rising smoke, a steam jet, cold mist that sinks, sparks, short bursts of electric sparks and falling dust (each a ParticleGenerator, the class of the game's steam vents, set as the game sets its own). Click one, then a floor, wall or ceiling: its cyan arrow comes out of the surface clicked, and that is the way the particles are thrown. Turn it with E, Rotation and Pitch.

While it is selected the particles play in the 3D view, with their texture: thrown along the arrow, rising or sinking, falling with gravity, growing and fading as the game does it. The Particles section of the right panel has its settings by name, each with a slider; the preview follows them as you drag:

  • Texture: the picture of each particle (its small picture opens the texture picker: the game's Effects package has its smoke, sparks and flames; the dropper takes one from the 3D view);
  • Chance and Every (s): how often it throws a puff; Particles each time: how many in a puff;
  • Speed along the arrow, Rise (below 0 they sink), Life (s), Size;
  • Spread out, Gravity, Grow, Fade out, Glow (unlit), Darkens (modulated).

Only the settings the game's class has are shown (read from the game the first time). always play the preview keeps it playing when it is not selected (the editor's only: the game does not read it). Generators already in a map (an original map's steam vents) have the same arrow, preview and section. The preview follows the game's rules closely, but it is not the game: Rebuild and Play show the particles for sure.

Particles that hurt, and switching them on and off. The Effects of the browser are UCParticles (DeusEdix's package: the game's particles, and more). Under the particle settings their section has:

  • Damage: how much each hit, its Kind (fire, electricity, poison gas, tear gas, radiation, EMP, explosion, shot, stun: what JC's augmentations and armour resist), its Radius round the stream, its Reach along the arrow (0: a ball round it), Hit every (s), and whether it Hurts characters too (else JC only). Nothing is hurt through a wall. While it is selected, a red see-through shape in the 3D view shows where it hurts;
  • Switching on and off: On at the start (off: it waits), Stays on (s) once switched on (0: until switched off), and whether "Switch on" also switches it off. Switch it with the Interactions' Switch on and Switch off (when the player uses a button, walks into a collision box, picks something up, a timer runs out...) or with the game's own triggers through its Tag. Off, it throws no more particles (those in the air finish their life) and its sound stops.

The browser has some ready: Steam that burns, Electric hazard, Toxic gas, Smoke when switched on (10 s). A generator of an original map (a ParticleGenerator) has Make it able to hurt and switch on/off: it becomes a UCParticles with the same particles (Replace with..., one Undo).

Your own effects. Save as my effect… in the section keeps the particles' settings (texture, damage and switching too) under a name of yours: they are in Objects > Effects of this project, under the game's, to place again in any map (right click one to delete it). A particle can have a picture of yours as its texture: import it into the map (the texture editor), then type its name in Texture.

Several objects at once

Ctrl+click or Shift+click adds an object to the selection (or takes it away) in the 3D view; in the Hierarchy, Ctrl+click adds one row and Shift+click picks every row in between. Ctrl+drag or Shift+drag in the 3D view selects every object inside the rectangle. The gizmo moves them together (W) and turns them about their middle (E), or about the last one picked with Pivot (Z). Delete, Ctrl+D, Alt+drag and End (drop to floor) act on the selection. Pieces are included in rectangle selection too. Objects, blocks and structures can be selected together; the panel lists them. If a protected member cannot be duplicated, the whole operation stops with a visible warning: it never silently copies only the lights. Un-nest to duplicate independent scenery, or use a reusable prefab. Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V can transfer several actors or blocks between maps; for mixed selections containing structures use Save as prefab instead. Alt+click away from a transform handle still selects a face.

Things stacked under the mouse (a light inside a lamp, a trigger inside a desk, a player start on a teleporter): click, then click again on the same spot to select the next one behind it, and so on round; the status bar says which one of how many (Under the pointer, 2 of 3: Teleporter). The first click picks the folder of the nearest thing, as usual; the next ones each thing on its own. The map's own walls are a step too (nothing selected). A click on the gizmo's handles moves the object instead: click beside them. Ctrl + right click (or right click > Select under cursor…) lists everything under the mouse with its icon, nearest first: click one to select it alone, Shift+click to add it to the selection or take it out. Hidden icons, hidden and locked things are not listed.

Selection commands (Edit menu): Select all (Ctrl+A) and Invert selection (Ctrl+I) work on what you can click in the view (hidden and locked things are left out), Deselect is Shift+D (or Esc), Cut (Ctrl+X) copies and deletes. Previous / Next selection (Ctrl+Shift+[ and Ctrl+Shift+], or the two arrows in the 3D view's toolbar) walk back and forth through the last 50 selections of the map, skipping what was deleted; after Esc, Previous brings the last selection back. Selection slots: Ctrl+Alt+1 to Ctrl+Alt+9 keep the selection (Edit > Keep selection in slot 1), Alt+1 to Alt+9 select it again; they are kept per map on this computer, with the camera bookmarks. In Face selection (Q) Alt+1 to Alt+9 and Alt+0 are the recent textures' (see Faces and texture alignment). These keys can be changed in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts, except the slots (so can Z, X and T of the gizmo and Aim at a point, Push to grid and the grid step's Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+], and the four commands of the selection and the view).

Mirror X / Y / Z (Edit menu, UnrealEd's Transform > Mirror) mirrors the selection about its middle, across the game's X, Y or Z axis. Blocks take their mirror image (a ramp rises the other way; a block with corners of its own, from Edit vertices, has them mirrored); walls, floors, stairs and roofs you drew too (on X or Y: a drawing stands on its floor), and curved or spiral stairs turn the other way. Objects face the mirrored way and keep their up (a model cannot be drawn mirrored); pieces of the map move to their mirrored place as they are. Their animations follow them. One Ctrl+Z undoes it.

With UnrealEd: Edit > Copy as T3D (UnrealEd) puts the selection on the clipboard the way UnrealEd copies actors, so Edit > Paste in UnrealEd puts it at the same place: blocks as brushes (a hole subtracts, a detail block is semisolid), what you drew as its pieces (cut by the holes, as the Rebuild cuts them), objects with their properties. The other way, what you copy in UnrealEd (Edit > Copy) comes in with Ctrl+V (or Edit > Paste T3D (UnrealEd)): brushes become blocks with corners of their own, the other actors objects, at the places they had in UnrealEd's map. A concave brush, a sheet, the builder brush and movers are left out and named in Messages (split a concave brush in UnrealEd first). The pieces of the map are UnrealEd's own brushes: copy them there.

Drop to floor and Stick to surface also work on independent Pieces and multi-selections. They use the actual geometry, ignoring the selected objects themselves. To place something while you drag it, hold Ctrl+Shift on the gizmo's middle square (see Snapping to corners and surfaces). If no reachable surface is found, the editor reports it without changing the object or adding an empty Undo step.

Editing several objects at once, as in Unity: with several things selected, the Inspector shows the fields they share. A field whose objects all have the same value shows it; where they differ it shows a dim —. Whatever you type or pick there is set on every one of them, and one Ctrl+Z takes it all back.

  • Transform: X, Y, Z, Rotation, Pitch, Roll and Scale %. A structure with doors or an elevator does not turn, and Scale % is for objects with a model (blocks and structures keep their size: resize them with R); a field only some of them have says how many (1 of 4). A block moved or turned keeps its size, and a structure carries its own objects (those selected with it are not moved twice).
  • Tags & events: the Engine tag and the Event of any objects, even mixed with blocks.
  • The Properties they all have: every field the classes of all of them have, by group, as one object shows them: when they are all lights, the Light section (colour, hue, saturation, brightness, radius, type, effect, cone...); for 8 doors, Locked, lock strength, opening time... Settings that belong to one object (accounts, emails, special options, inventory, relations, sounds, the conversation name) are listed under One object at a time: select one to set them.
  • All properties, when they are all of one class, each one's UnrealEd value, set on all of them.

The number fields take sums on each object's own value: +=16 in Y raises every crate by 16 from where it is, *=0.8 in Radius makes every light reach 80% as far as it did. L(a,b) spreads the objects evenly from a to b in the order of the list at the top of the Inspector (the order you picked them): L(0,512) in X lines up five lights 128 apart, L(0,270) in Rotation fans four chairs out. R(a,b) gives each a random value between a and b: R(0,360) in Rotation turns 20 trash cans each its own way. The status bar says what each one got (Rotation (yaw) in degrees: R(0,360) = 12 to 347 on 20 objects). Dragging the label of a number field showing — moves each one from its own value (the field shows +=8 while you drag): 17 lights spread over the map all move 8 along X, each from where it is.

Right click a field showing —: Set all to Light3's value: 150, one choice for each different value (Unity's Set to Value of). Narrow the selection: when different kinds of things are selected, a button for each kind with how many there are (Light 12, Block 3) keeps only those.

UnrealEd's tools

What UnrealEd has for brushes and surfaces, on the editor's blocks and on the map's faces:

  • Top, Front and Side views (View menu, off at first): panes at the right of the 3D view. The wheel zooms at the cursor, a right or middle drag moves the view, a click picks, dragging the selection moves it on the pane's plane with the grid. Cut leaves out what is between the pane and the 3D camera (above it, in the Top view), so rooms show from inside.
  • Edit vertices (V, the Edit menu or the block's panel): move a block's corners one by one, as UnrealEd's vertex editing; it stays a convex shape. (V held while the gizmo moves something is vertex snapping instead: see Snapping to corners and surfaces.) Delete takes a corner away, Reset corners gives the block its box and shape again.

  • Look in the panel of a face (Face selection, Q): UnrealEd's surface properties, Unlit, Translucent, Masked, Modulated, Two-sided, Mirror, Invisible, Fake backdrop, Special lit, No smooth, Auto U Pan, Auto V Pan. The last two make the faces' texture slide by itself at their zone's speed, as the original maps' clouds and some water do (View > Effects shows it moving after a Rebuild). A box is ticked when every selected face has it, mixed when some do. Select same texture picks every face of the map with that texture, Replace in whole map gives them all another one.
  • All properties, the last section of an object's panel: every property UnrealEd's Properties window shows for its class, applied at Rebuild after the editor's own.
  • Select all of this class (Edit menu, or right click on an object).
  • Measure (M): click two points on the surfaces for the distance between them.
  • Camera bookmarks: Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 keep the view, 1 to 9 go back to it (with Alt: the selection slots, see Several objects at once).

Folders (nests): keep things together

Like a nest in Premiere: select some things (Ctrl+click, Ctrl+drag) and press Ctrl+G (or Edit > Nest, or right click > Create folder). They become one folder in the Hierarchy, at the top, and one thing in the 3D view: a click on any of them picks the whole folder, the gizmo moves and turns it all; a double click picks one thing inside it.

  • A new folder's name is ready to type: type it and press Enter, or click anywhere else to keep it (Esc keeps the old name). Later, F2, Enter or right click > Rename names it again.
  • The + at the top of the Hierarchy makes an empty Folder (inside the folder chosen, if any, else in the one new objects go in): drag things into it. An empty folder stays until you delete it (Del, or its bin).
  • New objects go here (right click a folder or a nest, as Unity's Set as Default Parent): from then on what you place from the Browser (objects, characters, models, pieces, prefabs, blocks, holes, water, portals, signs), paste (Ctrl+V, Paste T3D), draw (walls, roads, floors, ceilings, stairs, roofs), make with the Hierarchy's + or the 2D Shape Editor, a hole's light and a new cutscene camera go into that folder. It is shown in bold, and a small banner at the top of the Hierarchy names it: click the name to find the folder, × to stop (or right click it > New objects go to the top level). One per map, kept with the map's changes; Ctrl+Z undoes setting it. Renamed, it stays; deleted or un-nested, it stops. While the folder (or one around it) is hidden or locked with its eye or padlock, new things stay at the top level, so that they do not vanish or get stuck at once: the banner says hidden or locked. The + and the Shape Editor put things in the folder picked in the Hierarchy first. As anything in a folder, a click in the 3D view picks the whole folder (a double click picks one thing). A copy made with Ctrl+D stays beside its original, in its own folder, and Replace with keeps the object where it was. Fill AI paths, the points of a patrol route and the sky's suns are not put there.
  • Expand all and Collapse all (right click anywhere in the Hierarchy, or the Hierarchy panel's menu ⋯) open or close every folder. Alt+click a folder's arrow to open or close it with every folder inside it.

Cubes, wedges, cylinders, cones, spheres and domes

The Hierarchy's + also makes a Cube, a Wedge (a ramp, high at its +X side), a Cylinder, a Cone, a Sphere or a Dome (half a sphere on a flat base), 128 units wide, where the 3D view looks (standing on the floor there) and inside the folder chosen (else the one new objects go in). Move it (W), turn it any way (E), resize it (R: the coloured handles from the middle, or the dot of one side to move that side alone, see Drag handles; or Width, Depth and Height in the Inspector); Shape in the Inspector changes one into another.

The round ones work like UnrealEd's brush builders, with their settings in the Inspector:

  • Sides: how many sides round (3 to 64, 16 by default). 6 makes a hexagon, 8 an octagon.
  • Hollow: the thickness of the wall, 0 for solid. A hollow cylinder is a pipe or a tower, a hollow dome or sphere is a shell. The editor cuts it into convex pieces by itself.
  • Top % (cone): the radius at the top as a share of the bottom. 0 is a point, more a cut cone.
  • Align to side (cylinder, cone): a flat side facing +X instead of a corner.

Unlike UnrealEd's builders they stay blocks: resize the box and the shape stretches with it (an oval cylinder, an egg, a low dome), and you see the texture before the Rebuild.

Give it a texture like a wall: pick one in the Inspector's Texture field, or drag a texture thumbnail onto the block (or click the thumbnail, then the block). Until the next Rebuild the block shows its texture the way the game will map it; Rebuild puts it in the game. Kind makes it a detail block or a hole (a round hole with a cylinder); water, zone portals, moving blocks and signs are always boxes.

Dragging an object into or out of a Nest keeps its world position, rotation and scale. A drop hint names the destination; cycles, locked groups and unsupported structural moves are refused. One drop is one Undo step. A Nest containing lights or other fixed-size actors can move and rotate, but cannot be scaled as a whole.

  • Un-nest (Ctrl+Shift+G, right click, or the button in the Inspector) takes everything out again.
  • One thing at a time: drag it in the Hierarchy out of the nest (anywhere outside it), or onto another nest to move it there. Nests can hold other nests.
  • Delete on a nest deletes it with what it holds; Ctrl+D duplicates it as a new nest. A copy of one thing inside a nest stays in that nest, right after the original.
  • A nest is the editor's own way of keeping things together: the map itself does not change.

The structures of the map are nests too. Open one in the Hierarchy: its pieces (each brush of the script, each piece pasted from another level) and its objects are listed inside it.

  • Un-nest a structure (Ctrl+Shift+G, or the Un-nest button) to take it apart: its pieces become structures of their own, its objects become free. They show apart at once; Rebuild makes them separate structures of the map. What the map script describes under one comment stays together in a nest named after that comment, so a big structure comes apart one level at a time.
  • Drag one piece or one object out of a structure in the Hierarchy to take only that one out (right click > Take out of... does the same). On an object, the Inspector has take it out next to Part of.
  • Nothing jumps: what comes out stays exactly where it is now. Ctrl+Z puts it back.

Structures

A structure is a group of walls, lights and objects that belong together: the helipad, a building, a staircase. Click one of its walls, or pick it in the Hierarchy.

  • Move and rotate it with the gizmo or the numbers; its lights and objects follow.
  • Scale stretches it, one axis at a time if you want. Textures repeat over the bigger surface, unless you tick stretch textures.
  • Copy, Duplicate, Reset (put it back as it was), Delete.
  • Structures with doors or lifts can be moved and rotated; their native motion keys follow. Their size remains protected from scaling.
  • Un-nest takes it apart into its pieces and objects (see Nests above).

In existing maps, moving pieces of the map makes the rebuild recalculate geometry and lighting: check the result with View in game. Some original maps cannot be rebuilt (Wan Chai Street, for example): there you can only change objects, not the map's geometry.

Adding things

  • Objects (the browser at the bottom): search any object of the game ("crate", "barrel", "lamp", "guard"...), pick it, then click a floor or a wall to place it. Shift+click places more copies.
  • B adds a block: a wall or a platform with the texture you choose; tick solid to make it walkable.
  • Pieces (the browser): fences, chain-link, grates, railings, pipes, vents, cables, signs and neon, lamps, windows, ladders, beams and columns, wall and floor panels, shelves, awnings... In Deus Ex these are not objects but brushes (geometry) the level designers made: the editor takes them from the original levels, each one whole (the brushes that make it, nothing around it) and only once even if the game repeats it a hundred times. The tile says how often the game uses it and where. Click one, then a floor (it stands on it) or a wall (its front faces out): it becomes part of the map with Rebuild, like a pasted structure; move, turn and copy it as one. The first time, the Pieces tab reads the game's original maps to find them (Generate Pieces): several maps at once, a few minutes in all; after that only changed maps are read again.
  • Game doors and elevators are in Objects → Movers, under Doors or Elevators · two stops. Pieces contains static geometry; Movers contains native moving brushes. Choose a textured preview, place it in an opening and Rebuild. Doors are individual leaves/panels; use them in Play to open and close. Lifts start when the player steps on, wait four seconds at the other stop, then return. Leave room for their original travel, including space below a descending lift. Geometry, textures, movement and assigned sounds come from your selected installed game. Each placement has an independent tag; original locks, keys, mission events, return groups and external controllers are removed. Immediately after placement the Inspector identifies it as a DeusExMover, with activation, move/wait times and door locking. These settings are saved and support Undo and independent duplicates. After Rebuild, Edit mover… selects the native actor for its tags, interactions, Door sounds and Animate settings. Moving, turning, duplicating and Undo work as with a piece. A mover placed by an older version can be selected as a native actor after Rebuild. Multi-floor/script-controlled mechanisms and unsupported tilted movers are not listed as ready-made pieces. This does not cut a doorway or build an elevator shaft, buttons or navigation automatically. On upgrade, Refresh scan adds the mover library using existing reference caches where possible; its first pass also extracts missing mover previews locally. No game assets are shipped.
  • Prefabs (the browser): select a structure or a nest and choose Save as prefab… (in the Inspector, the right-click menu or the Prefabs tab). It is kept as it is now, built, with the 3D view as its picture, and can be placed in any level of the project. Doors, lifts, mission objects and objects with an Event are still left out of a prefab (their tags would have to be renamed for every copy). Linked copies, as Unity's prefab instances: a copy placed from the Prefabs tab stays linked to its prefab. Its row in the Hierarchy has a v2 badge (the version it places; yellow with ↑: a newer version is in the library; dashed: the prefab was deleted from it). Its Inspector has a Prefab section with the prefab, the version and its overrides: what you changed on that copy's objects (moved, deleted, given another class with Replace with..., properties changed, off in game; a click on one selects that object). Duplicate and Copy / Paste of a linked copy take its overrides along, moved with the new copy. Its buttons, also in the copy's right-click menu, each one step of the History (a version saved by Apply to prefab stays in the library when you undo it: the copy goes back to the version it had):
  • Select instances: every copy of that prefab in the map.
  • Apply to prefab: the copy, as it is built, becomes the newest version of its prefab (Rebuild first, as for Save as prefab); its overrides are the prefab's from then on. A copy scaled away from 100% is not applied (a prefab keeps its own size), nor one on an older version: update it first, or it would undo the versions since.
  • Update instances: every copy of that prefab in this map goes to the newest version, each where it is, with its overrides (a change to an object the new version no longer has goes; Messages says which). Rebuild shows them. Copies in other maps keep their version until you update them there.
  • Unpack (several copies at once from the right-click menu): the copy forgets its prefab and stays as it is.
  • A prefab's card in the Prefabs tab, its ⋯ (or a right click): Select instances and Update instances in the open map, Rename… (every version of it; copies already built show the new name after the next Rebuild), Use the current view as its picture, Delete…: asked first, with the maps and map templates that place it. Its versions leave the library but stay on disk (copies are built from them, and Undo or the clipboard can place one again); they go when the project is next opened after DeusEdix was closed or another project was open, if no saved map or template places them, and a copy pasted after that is refused, saying so (only when its files are really gone: when the editor cannot find out, the copy is pasted). The card says the prefab's version and how many copies the open map has (and how many are older).
  • Walls, roads, floors and ceilings: draw them (see Drawing walls, roads, floors and ceilings below), or use + Block (B) with the game's wall and floor textures, or start from a room (New map).
  • Right click a folder in the Hierarchy > New objects go here: what you add from then on goes into it (see Folders (nests)).

Drawing walls, roads, floors and ceilings

Stairs and roofs from A to B

Textures

The Textures section of the right panel lists the pictures of the selected object (one per skin) or structure (one per texture of its faces). Edit opens it in the Texture Editor; Import opens a picture of yours (PNG, JPG...) there; Export downloads the PNG for another paint program; Original goes back to the game's texture.

Choosing a texture: every texture field (a block's, a hole's, a drawn wall's...) has a small picture of its texture next to it. Click the picture: a grid opens with the textures of this map, All textures of the game (search by name) and every package. A click tries a texture at once, a double click (or Enter) keeps it, Esc closes. The pictures of a package are made from your game the first time you open it (a few seconds), then they are kept. You can still type a name in the field. Right click a picture > Find in map: the objects, blocks and structures of the map that use that texture (the Hierarchy's search tex=, see Searching the map). Faces and texture alignment

Alt+click a wall, floor or ceiling to select its face. Shift+Alt+click adds or removes faces. Edit > Face selection (Q, configurable in Settings) lets you select faces with plain clicks and add them with Shift. In fly mode, Q still moves down. The selected faces are highlighted and the right panel shows Surface. Selecting an object returns to its normal inspector.

Choose a loaded Texture and press Apply, or use Align:

AlignmentResult
Floor / WallReset the mapping to horizontal / vertical alignment
One TileFit one complete texture to the source face's width and height
One Tile U / VFit the width / height of one tile and preserve the texture's proportions
Around a cylinderWrap one tile continuously around all vertical side faces of the brush; the caps stay unchanged

Cylinder sides must form one closed outline and use the same texture. Pan U/V adds whole texels to the offset. Scale U/V multiplies the tile size (2 is twice as large, 0.5 is half); a Pan of 0, 0 or a Scale of 1, 1 changes nothing and is not queued. Slip starts the Texture Slip (below). Rotate 90°, Flip U and Flip V change its orientation. Select whole brush selects all its visible faces; a structure's Surfaces section can select the faces of all its brushes.

Recent textures, as ProBuilder's material palette: under the Texture list the panel keeps the last 10 textures you applied to faces (with the list, the texture picker or a drag), newest first, each with its picture and its key, in the project. Click one, or in Face selection press Alt+1 to Alt+9 and Alt+0, to put it on the selected faces: one Ctrl+Z takes it back. A texture put from the strip keeps its place there, so its key stays the same while you texture a room (floor, walls, trim, ceiling); one applied another way comes first. In Face selection these keys are the strip's, not the selection slots' (those work again once Face selection is off); they can be changed in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts (Face selection). While a Rebuild runs they wait for it.

Grow (Alt+G, Edit > Grow face selection), as ProBuilder's Grow Selection, adds the faces that share an edge with the selected ones and lie in their plane (within 5°): a wall or a floor split by BSP cuts, or one that goes on through a doorway, is selected with a press or two before Align. Grow (any angle) (Alt+Shift+G) adds every face next to them: the walls round a floor, the sides of a column. Each press adds one ring of faces; a piece of an edge is enough (BSP cuts meet at T-junctions), a corner is not. It takes the faces as the 3D view shows them: hidden objects are left out, a structure moved since the Rebuild counts where it is now. The keys work in Face selection (Q); the menu items and the panel's Grow buttons whenever faces are selected.

Texture Slip (Y, Edit > Slip the texture, the Surface panel's Slip), as Premiere's slip tool: with faces selected (in Face selection or with Alt+click), point at one of them and press Y (pressed elsewhere, or from the menu or the panel, it starts when the pointer comes over one). Move the pointer: the texture rolls on the faces in any direction, the texel under the pointer staying under it, on walls, floors, ceilings and slopes alike. The wheel makes it larger (up) or smaller round the point under the pointer: four notches double it. Shift is finer (an eighth of the pointer's way, a sixteenth of a doubling a notch), Ctrl snaps the pan to an eighth of the tile and the size to whole powers of two, the arrows nudge it a texel the way they point on the screen (with Ctrl, a snap step). The line beside the pointer says the pan and the size. A click (or letting go after a drag) or Enter keeps it: one Ctrl+Z (Slip the texture in the History) takes it back, Rebuild puts it in the game. Esc, the right button, Alt, a click elsewhere or a menu, leaving the editor, Undo, Save, a Rebuild, closing or opening a map cancel it, with nothing changed.

It queues what the panel's Scale and Pan do: one Scale for all the faces and each face its own Pan, so a texture running over several faces stays one: the faces stay within half a texel of the face under the pointer (exact when made smaller by whole factors, such as Ctrl to ×1/2), any two within a texel, however many slips of the same faces you keep one after another. Each slip starts where the slips before it left the faces, to a fraction of a texel, and the History keeps that with each step: an Undo, a Redo or a jump in the History gives it back with the changes, a Rebuild keeps it and a Pan of a face moves it as much. A face changed otherwise since starts from its queued pans, in whole texels: an alignment, One Tile, Rotate or a flip queued on it, Around a cylinder queued on its brush (not on the caps, which it leaves as they are) or a Scale; so does every face after Revert all changes, after the editor is opened again and in another map (and see the known limits below). What the assistant's scripts write into the queued changes directly counts as the panel's: an alignment, One Tile, Around a cylinder (on the faces it wraps), Rotate or flip added on a face or taken off it drops its fraction, and anything else is judged by the numbers alone (a face still where the slip left it, give or take whole texels, keeps its fraction). Faces joined by edges where the texture runs on round them (a ring of walls, the sides of a column, a box's top over the side its texture comes from) scale as one; a face whose texture runs on round none of its edges with the others, such as a floor or a cap aligned otherwise, scales round its own point under the pointer. Scaling a texture wrapped right round a closed shape, such as a cylinder, keeps every edge closed but one. A ring parts first where two textures meet (walls going round through a floor of another texture part at the floor's edge, not at a corner of the walls), then where its texture already steps (by more than a texel), else at its shortest edge, and a slip kept after another parts it again where that one did while its texture still steps there, even where another of its edges steps, with a Rebuild, an Undo, a Redo, a jump in the History, a Pan of all the same faces or a structure moved and rebuilt between them too; an edge lined up since, by rolling one face alone or a Pan, counts as one that does not step, so the ring parts where its texture still steps (with every edge closed it must still part somewhere, perhaps at an edge you lined up); a ring of equal edges of one texture parts at the one farthest round from the face under the pointer (from the side nearest the pointer when it is on a face apart, such as a cap), so point at the side that must stay seamless. The same goes for a face set in another, such as a sign in a wall, whose texture runs on over two of its sides: a side where the texture already steps parts before one where it does not, and of two alike, the one nearest the pointer stays closed. A pan wraps round the texture only past the engine's ±32767 texels. Faces only a Rebuild shows (a change queued on them the view cannot work out, BSP pieces mapped otherwise) are left out, and the line says how many. One slip adds at most 512 of the map's 4096 queued surface changes, and a slip of the same faces folds into the pans and scales before it; for a large selection (Select same texture on a big map) slip fewer faces, or press P: every face then gets the pan of the one under the pointer (two changes in all), and seams may show between the others. With fewer than 1024 changes left, the line and the status bar say how many. It refuses faces of a locked structure, blocks not built yet, a structure resized since the Rebuild, and a selection over structures moved since the Rebuild otherwise than the face under the pointer (Rebuild first).

The Texture Slip's known limits:

  • A Scale brought back to where it was (×2, then ×1/2) counts as none: the next slip still starts from the fraction of a texel the slip before left, as if both Scales were undone.
  • Rolling a part of a ring alone moves its texture against the rest of the ring at both ends of that part.
  • A Pan of only some of a ring's faces between two slips: the next slip may part the ring at another edge.
  • After a structure is moved and rebuilt between two slips, its faces start from their queued pans, in whole texels, so an edge may end up about a texel off.
  • A structure resized (Scale) or turned and rebuilt between two slips you kept can open a second seam in a ring. To avoid it, finish the slips, or Undo them, before you resize or turn the structure.
  • A Rotate or a Flip U queued on a ring's faces between two slips you kept can open a second seam at the next slip. One Tile, Wall or Floor queued between them may make it part the ring at another edge.

These operations affect the full source face, including pieces split by BSP cuts. They are stored in order and the 3D view shows them at once, as the game will, but for these, which show only after a Rebuild (the panel says how many of the selected faces): Around a cylinder; a texture none of the map's surfaces or objects use yet, its size known only after a Rebuild; a face whose BSP pieces are mapped otherwise; Floor or Wall on a structure moved or turned since the Rebuild; any change on a structure resized since the Rebuild; an alignment or a texture taken back after a Rebuild; and, on a map last rebuilt by an older editor, the changes it had when opened. Save keeps them, Rebuild puts them in the map and its lighting. A change Save cannot keep is said when the map opens; one the Rebuild would fail on (a pan past the engine's 16 bits, One Tile on a face with no size) is said at once and again after Save, and Rebuild asks before it starts. Undo (Ctrl+Z) removes the last edit. Changing UVs also rebuilds lighting because the game's lightmaps use those coordinates. Changes are written to both the rendered surfaces and their source brushes, so later geometry rebuilds keep them. If the source brush or its face layout is replaced outside the editor, select the new faces again.

Shadow detail

In Surface > Shadows, choose Ultra Low, Low, Normal or High. High makes finer shadows and uses more map memory; Low is coarser. Ultra Low gives the coarsest shadows and smallest lightmaps, useful for distant surfaces. It uses UnrealEd's combined Low + High shadow detail flags; Normal clears both flags. A selected structure also has Shadows for the whole structure, which includes every face of its brushes.

Rebuild recalculates lighting. On an existing map, shadow or UV edits alone do not rebuild geometry or AI paths. Messages reports the lightmap sample count and shadow storage before and after the build. The size difference depends on the faces and lights in the map. View > Lightmap detail shows the result across the map: every surface in the colour of how far apart its samples are.

Interactions (when this happens, do that)

No Tag or Event to write: at the top of an object's Properties, its Interactions say in plain words what happens when something happens to it.

  • What it offers: first, two rows of buttons. When it... lists what can happen to that object (each with the number of its rules): click one to add a rule on it. It can... lists what a rule can make it do (with the number of rules that do): click one, then the object that should make it happen. Both come from the object's class, the way the game itself works: a keypad offers the right code is typed, a beam JC breaks its beam, a camera nothing of its own (the game gives it no event). Things the game cannot do are not offered: an elevator is not switched on, a trigger that only redirects others (an alliance or an orders trigger) does not start rules.
  • + When it...: what starts it. A door: it has opened, it starts closing, it is blown up. A button or any object the player uses: the player uses it. A keypad: the right code is typed, a wrong code is typed. A computer: JC chooses an option of it (write the text of the button on its screen and what the screen says after). A trigger zone: JC walks in. A laser: JC breaks its beam. An alarm: it sounds the alarm. A character: they are killed or knocked out. An item: JC picks it up. An object: it is destroyed. Most things: JC comes near (within a distance you choose).
  • + Then...: something to an object (click it): click it in the 3D view or in the Hierarchy, and the list shows only what that object can do:
  • doors: open, close, open or close, lock, unlock, blow up, give JC its key;
  • characters: attack JC (alone: their faction stays out of it), turn hostile, become friendly or neutral, stand still, wander about, follow JC, walk or run to a place (click it), leave or come back (only when JC does not see them);
  • lights: turn on, turn off, turn on or off;
  • elevators: go to a floor (0 is where it starts);
  • cameras, turrets, alarms, lasers: switch on, switch off; a sound effect: play it;
  • objects, items, bodies, keypads, computers and alarm lights: vanish, appear again;
  • most devices and objects (not characters, lights or lasers): an explosion there (damage and radius); most things: a sound from it.

Or without an object: a message on the screen, a goal (the same name completes it later), a note in the DataVault, a flag (the Story's flags: an interaction can start a Story event), a counter (add to it, take from it, or set it: the levers pulled, the guards down), a sound (from an object or in JC's head), take JC to another map.

  • + Only if...: conditions, all of which must hold when it happens, or the rule does nothing that time (it is not used up, even with once): a state (a flag) is yes or no, JC carries an item (or does not), a character is alive (not killed nor knocked out), JC's skill is at least Trained, Advanced or Master, a counter compared with a number, JC's credits. The Story's events take the same conditions. The conditions are checked the moment it happens, before any rule acts: if one rule on a button adds 1 to a counter, another rule on the same button still sees the old number that time.
  • Every action has its after ... s: seconds from the moment the rule starts (the door opens, 2 s later the alarm sounds). once: only the first time.
  • 🔗 Connect to...: click another object, and the rule is made with what the two usually do together: a button or a keypad opens a door, a zone sends a guard at JC, a switch turns a light on or off. The object that can start something acts on the other; change what it does afterwards.
  • Each rule is also written as a sentence ("When the player uses button UCLButton: open door UCLDoor, turn off light UCLLamp, after 1 s the message ..."). Green arrows in the 3D view go from the object to what it acts on; the Properties of that object list the rules acting on it (click one to go to its object).
  • Far away or out of view: when the other end of an arrow is off the screen (or behind you), a green label on the edge of the view points towards it, with its name and distance (to light Light114 · 307 m, or from ... for an object acting on this one); click it and the camera goes there. An object in view but very far gets the label just above it. both next to an action frames the object and the one it acts on together. A rule that takes JC to another map shows takes JC to ... by the object.
  • Logic (top bar): every interaction of the map, grouped by object and editable there too, like an event sheet. + When the map starts is for what happens at once (a message, a goal, a guard that leaves).
  • Check lists rules whose objects were deleted, actions without an object, sounds not chosen, texts left empty, maps that do not exist.

Rebuild puts them in the game. The game's own Tag and Event (Connections (Tag / Event)) still work: an object with an Event of its own keeps triggering it as well.

When JC presses a key

In the Logic window, + When JC presses a key makes a rule that runs when JC presses the key you choose (a letter, a digit, F1–F12, Insert, Delete, Home, End, Page Up/Down, the numeric keypad). It works in Play: the key is bound for that launch in Play's own copy of your settings (yours are left as they are). A key the game already uses (W, A, S, D, E, F, the number keys...) is taken from JC for that launch: the rule says so in red.

First-person moments (the weapon JC holds)

Tools > First-person moments... makes moments of the weapon JC holds, seen as the player sees it: an inspection of the gun, a gesture when a weapon is picked up, a weapon raised as a door opens.

  • Weapon: the one it is made for (its first-person model and its place in the view are read from the game). In the game the moment plays only while JC holds that weapon.
  • Clips: pieces of the weapon's own animations one after another (Select, Reload, Shoot...): any frames of one, at any speed (in ... s), backwards too (⇄, or a to before the from). The last frame stays until the moment ends.
  • Moves: keys of the weapon's place in the view (forward, right, up), straight from one to the next; the last one is where it ends (0 0 0: back where it was).
  • Sounds at their seconds, and a shake of the view (in the game only).
  • ▶ and the timeline play it in the view, with its sounds.

An Interaction plays it: JC's weapon plays a moment (an action without an object), when JC presses a key, uses a button, walks into a collision box, picks something up... JC firing, reloading or putting the weapon away ends the moment at once.

New animations from Blender. Export the model (.glb) writes the weapon's first-person model (arms and gun) at rest. Animate it in Blender any way you like (an armature, shape keys) without moving its points in edit mode, and export it as .glb with its animation. Import an animation (.glb)... reads it back frame by frame (30 frames a second, or the number you choose) as a new animation of the weapon, FP_<name>, in a copy of its model in DeusEdix's DeusEdixAnims package (as the Animation Studio's), and adds its clip to the moment. In the game the weapon wears that copy while the moment plays.

Scripting tags

Select an object and open Inspector > Scripting tag. A scripting tag groups objects for new interactions in the current map. Several objects can share one tag; it is not a unique object ID, a faction or a Story flag.

Auto, Custom and Original

  • Auto follows the object's type or current state. Characters use neutral, friendly or enemy according to their attitude towards JC. Lights use the nearest major colour: red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple, magenta or pink; very low colour saturation becomes white. Brightness and switching a light off do not change its colour tag.
  • Typing a tag makes it Custom. JOHN stays JOHN when you change the character's faction, attitude or appearance. Even typing the same value as the automatic tag makes it manual. Auto explicitly returns it to automatic behaviour.
  • Objects with an authored native tag start in Original mode, keeping that tag as their scripting label. Auto opts into automatic labels; Original, when available, restores the inherited label after an override.

Use 1-64 ASCII letters, digits or underscores, starting with a letter or underscore: JOHN_2, not John Smith. Matching ignores case but matches the whole tag: GREEN matches green, not greenish.

Target a tag or filter a group

In an object's Interactions, add a starting event, then choose + Then... > objects with a tag…. Enter the Tag and choose the action. A light action affects matching lights, not characters that happen to have the same tag. The match count is a preview of the current map; automatic tags can change before the action runs in the game.

For a local group, choose + Then... > a group within a radius…. Set Group to Lights, enter green in Tag, and choose the Radius and Center. An empty Tag accepts all tags. Refresh group captures the current matching objects. Saved members remain connected if moved; Include newcomers also adds matching objects in the zone when the action runs. The tag filter is checked at execution, and the radius reaches through walls: it does not detect room boundaries.

Try this with a green light, a red light and a button: use the button's the player uses it event, target green, choose the action that turns lights off, then Rebuild and test in the game. Only matching lights should switch off. Save and Rebuild after changing tags or their interactions.

Native tags and story safety

Scripting tag is separate from Engine tag (legacy), the native Event and the character's BindName. A scripting override does not rename these identities or reconnect the original campaign. This applies to every character, including Anna, not just newly placed NPCs. Do not change an engine tag merely to put an object in a new scripting group.

A tag selects recipients; a Story flag remembers what happened. Assigning JOHN does not make Anna an ally, kill her or rewrite her later appearances. Changing AI, alliances, dialogue or character outcomes can still affect the campaign. Use Mission graph to inspect the recorded dependencies before making those changes.

Factions

Every character belongs to a faction (UNATCO, Cop, triad_red for the Red Arrow, triad_lum for the Luminous Path, mj12...) and has relations: how they treat JC (Player) and each other faction when the map starts, friendly, neutral or hostile. Members of the same faction are always friends. A relation can be fixed: it never changes by itself. If it is not fixed, it works as in the game: attack them and they turn hostile.

  • A character's relations: select them; in Properties set Faction and edit the Relations list (faction, attitude, fixed). The field suggests every faction of the game and of the map.
  • The Factions panel (top bar) lists the factions of the map, how many characters each has (click the number to see and select them) and how they treat JC. Change a whole faction sets a relation for all its members at once, for example Red Arrow towards JC becomes hostile.
  • Window > Characters table lists every character with its faction, how it treats JC and its relations: sort or filter by them, and change Towards JC or the faction of one or of the selected rows (see Tables of lights, doors and characters).
  • During the mission (after a dialogue, when a flag is set, when JC reaches a point) use the Story: the action A faction... turns a whole faction friendly, neutral or hostile towards JC or another faction; A character... joins a faction moves one character; the event a faction turns hostile to JC reacts when that happens (JC shot one of them, or the story turned them).
  • Only one character: A character... becomes hostile to JC (or attacks JC right now). Tick alone to keep the rest of their faction out of it: in the game, someone who attacks calls his friends for help, and characters treat neutrals as friends, so without alone the others may join the fight. With alone the character leaves their faction and does not call for help. If JC then fires a gun in front of the others, they may still react to the shooting, as in the game.

To place a character: Objects (the browser), search its name ("Red Arrow", "MJ12", "civilian", "dog"...), click the floor; then set its orders, faction and relations in Properties. To make a character of your own, see the next section.

Characters

The Character Creator (Characters in the top bar, or Characters... in the browser) makes characters of your own from the bodies, faces and clothes of the game's characters.

  1. + New, or Start from a character of the game: it takes their whole look, their behaviour and their weapons.
  2. Body: the kind of body (man in a long coat, man in a suit, man in a uniform, woman in a dress, heavy man, child...). The pieces it shares with the old one stay: a face, the trousers.
  3. Look: every piece of the body (Face, Top, Legs, Long coat, Glasses, Hat / helmet...) has the versions that exist in the game. The arrows go through them, the button with the number opens them all as pictures; under it, which character of the game wears it. Faces fit every body of the same kind (all the men, all the women), trousers every body with the same legs. Random face and Random clothes mix them for you.
  4. Behaviour: Civilian (goes about their business, runs away from fights), Soldier (guards, takes cover, fights back), Criminal (attacks when provoked). When placed: stands, walks around, sits, dances. Health (100 is normal).
  5. Faction and Towards JC (friendly, neutral, hostile, fixed), as in Factions above. To make a faction of your own (a new gang), give the same faction name to all its characters.
  6. Carries: up to four weapons or items (a weapon needs its ammo too).
  7. Save. The preview turns: drag it to turn it, the wheel zooms.

The character is now the first entry of Objects in the browser: click it and click a floor, as many times as you want (each copy can still change its own properties in the Inspector). Place in map does the same from the Creator. If you change a saved character and save it again, its copies already placed in the open map change too (save the map and Rebuild).

Characters are files in tools\mapkit\characters\ (one .json each): Export saves one anywhere, to share it; Import loads one made by someone else. Duplicate makes a variant.

In the game they keep exactly the look you gave them (the HDTP models do not replace it), and their corpse wears the same clothes, also after JC carries it and puts it down. They have the walk, eye height and pain sounds of the body's original character (a woman's body has a woman's voice). They say nothing by themselves: give them dialogues in the Story (they have their own conversation name, UCEd_<their name>).

The first time, the Creator reads the characters of the game (a few seconds). Characters need DeusEdix's classes UCCustomCivilian, UCCustomSoldier, UCCustomThug: they are part of its package, so Install puts them in the game together with the map.

Animation: any object, doors and elevators too

Select an object (a model, a decoration, a light, a door, an elevator, a block) and Animate...: the Animation panel opens at the bottom, like Premiere's Motion or Unity's Animation window. It has rows for Position, Rotation and Scale, with their values at the playhead and their keys on the timeline.

  • Move the playhead (click or drag on the timeline), then move, turn or scale the object with the gizmo (W, E, R) or type its values: every change is a key at that time (the first one also puts a key at 0, where it rests). ◆ adds or takes away a key of that row; drag a key to move it in time; click a key to choose its curve (linear, smooth, ease in, ease out, hold) or to delete it.
  • ▶ plays it (the gizmo hides while it plays), ◀ plays it backwards, ■ goes back to the start. Once, Loop or Back and forth; at the start: it plays when the map starts.
  • Positions are offsets from where the object stands, so moving the object moves its whole animation. Rotation in degrees: 360 is a whole turn (two keys 0 and 360: it spins once).
  • Doors, elevators and moving blocks are the engine's movers: up to 8 keys, no scale, and whoever stands on them moves with them. A door you animate still opens when the player uses it, with your movement (a door that slides can swing on its hinge instead). A block becomes a moving block (Kind in its panel, or Animate... on it) and moves in the game as you animate it: a lift, a platform, a crusher.
  • In the Interactions: Then... → Play its animation, Play it backwards, Stop its animation, and When → its animation has finished to chain things. Each rule has a Timeline: its actions as bars at their time (an animation as long as it lasts); drag a bar to when it should happen.
  • Events (the last row, like Unity's Animation Events): things that happen at a time of the animation, in step with it however it is retimed: a clank when a crane arm stops, a hiss as a panel slides open, an alarm when a lift reaches a floor. + puts one at the playhead: Play a sound (the sound list opens; heard from the object, within its radius, at its volume) or Trigger an Event (type a Tag: every object with that Tag is triggered, a door, a light, an alarm, a sound effect). Or drop a sound from the browser on the row: a sound event at that time. Click a marker to change it or delete it, drag it to retime it (up to 16). In the game they happen whenever the animation passes them: every round of a Loop, both ways of Back and forth, played backwards; once each time (where it turns back, an event there plays once), and a door's or an elevator's whoever moves it (JC opening a door too). An event after the last key happens at the end. ▶ plays the sound events as the playhead passes them, and dragging the playhead forwards plays them too (backwards it does not); an Event only says in the status bar what it will trigger. To run an Interaction at a time, trigger the Tag of something whose own rule reacts (a sound effect, a door).

Collision boxes

Objects > Triggers > Collision box: a box (purple in the 3D view; its Width, Depth and Height in its Properties, or drag the dot of one side in the 3D view: see Drag handles in Objects). In its Interactions: JC walks in and JC walks out; Anyone, not only JC counts the characters too; Only once; a Message to JC. Tick Solid and it is an invisible wall (orange): JC cannot walk through it (Rebuild makes the wall), and JC walks in is when he touches it, to keep him in an area or tell him why he cannot go on.

Cutscenes

A cutscene is a camera that flies along keys on a timeline, like the camera paths of a video editor or of the Minecraft film mods: the player watches, then gets JC back where he was.

  1. Look at the first view you want and press + Cutscene (bottom bar): a Cutscene camera is made, its first key is that view, and its timeline opens at the bottom with 🎥 Look through on (the 3D view becomes the game's screen: 16:9, with the black bars).
  2. Click the timeline further on (the red playhead), fly the camera there (right mouse + WASD, the mouse to turn) and press K (or ◆ Key). Each key keeps the camera's place, where it looks, its Roll (tilt) and its Zoom (field of view: smaller = closer). Do it for every key.
  3. ▶ (or Space) plays it in the editor: the picture as the game shows it, black bars, subtitles, fades. ■ goes back to the start; Esc stops, then stops looking through.
  4. The camera flies through the keys without slowing down at them (a curve in time, as in video editors): it starts gently at the first key and stops gently at the last. Click a key to change what it does there: Smooth (passes through), Stop here (slows down to it and starts again), Linear (straight to the next key at the same speed), Hold (stays until the next key, then jumps); its Zoom, Roll, Go there, Set to the view (the key becomes what you see now), Delete key. Drag a key along the timeline to change its time: keys far apart in space but close in time make the camera fast there.
  5. Path: Smooth through the keys (a curve that does not stop at each key) or Straight lines. Even speed spaces the keys so the camera keeps the same speed all along. Looks at: a character the camera always turns to (the keys still give its place, roll and zoom).
  6. ✂ New shot cuts at the playhead: the shot so far ends there and a new one starts with the view you have (fly elsewhere and press K for its first key). Between two shots the film jumps, no flight. Join with the shot before takes a cut away.
  7. Subtitles: + at the playhead, then Who (a name, in yellow), the line, how long (0: as long as the text needs), a sound or a voice. Drag them along the timeline.
  8. Events: + at the playhead, then click a door, a character, a light... in the 3D view or the Hierarchy: it happens at that second (the door opens at 3.2 s, the guard runs off). They are the Interactions When it starts of the cutscene (in its Properties, where you can add messages, sounds, goals): drag them along the timeline to change their second. When it has ended is in its Properties too (a guard attacks, JC goes to another map).
  9. Options: Fade in / out (seconds from and to black), black bars, can be skipped (a click or Space skips it: what was still to happen at once), only once, at the start of the map, JC visible where he stands (a stand-in of JC stays there while the camera flies), Length.
  10. 👁 Preview (on at first): as the playhead moves or plays, the events show on the objects as the game does them by then. A door or an elevator opens through its keys in its MoveTime, after its DelayTime (a door that closes by itself does after its StayOpenTime; Go to a floor goes straight to that key); an object plays its animation, backwards, or stops; a character walks or runs to its place at its own speed, with its walk animation (along the AI paths of the last Rebuild; sent on the walkable area, or with no AI paths, straight there); a light goes dark (its icon, and its light in the Lit view with View > Effects on); something vanishes or appears again. Move the playhead back before an event and the object is where it is again. Nothing is changed in the map: no undo step, nothing to save. A click or a key outside the panel puts everything back first, so that what you do starts from where things really are; move the playhead to see them again. Events the editor cannot show (a message, a sound, a lock, an attack...) are dashed on the timeline: Rebuild and Play for those. A subtitle with a sound shows its voice's waveform in its clip, as long as the voice lasts, and ▶ plays the voices in time from where the playhead is, as the game does (a line's voice starts when the line shows, and plays to its end). 👁 Preview off: the objects stay where they are and the voices are silent.

What plays it: an interaction Play the cutscene (select a collision box, 🔗 Connect to..., click the camera: When JC walks into it: play the cutscene), a button, a door, a character killed...; a Story event or dialogue (action Play a cutscene: under a flag, after a talk); or at the start of the map. A cutscene that starts during a conversation waits for its end.

In the game JC does not move, cannot be hurt and is not seen while it plays; the stories of the map and the collision boxes wait. At the end he is back where he was, looking where he looked, with his weapon. Check map warns about a cutscene with no keys or one nothing plays.

Models: every 3D model, and your own

The Models tab of the browser (next to Objects and Pieces) lists every 3D model the game can load, by package: Deus Ex's (DeusExDeco, DeusExCharacters, DeusExItems), HDTP's, Revision's, the packages of other mods (Packages...) and yours (DeusEdixModels). Search by name; a package shows its previews the first time it is opened (a few seconds). Click a model, then a floor or a wall: it is placed as an object of its own (DeusEdix's UCMeshDeco) with a collision round it, at the size the game uses it. Its Properties: Model (Replace…: click another one in the Models tab), name, solid, cannot be destroyed, strength, JC can push it, and the Interactions of any object. Right click a model > Find in map: the objects of the map that have it (the Hierarchy's search mesh=, see Searching the map).

Replace a model, keep the logic: select a mesh object, then Inspector > Model > Replace…. Click the replacement in Models (including imported models). This changes the mesh and its skins, not the actor's class or name: tags, links, AI, dialogue, inventory, transform, pivot and collision stay. The old per-object texture overrides are removed with the old model. Cancel or Esc leaves it unchanged; Ctrl+Z restores the entire replacement, including those textures. Rebuild applies it in game. The appearance is reapplied once after the initial HDTP setup; later gameplay effects remain controlled by the original class. Characters need compatible animation sequences and attachments; corpses and inventory/first-person models still follow the original class. Brush doors/elevators and map structures are not mesh actors, so they do not offer this command. To change the class too (a crate into a model, a model into a barrel), use Replace with… (see Objects).

Import model... (Models tab) makes your own: a model made in Blender, 3ds Max, Maya... saved as glTF / GLB (best: its textures inside), FBX, OBJ (with its .mtl) or DAE. Choose the file together with its textures (Ctrl+click). The preview shows it next to JC (about 90 units tall); set its height in units (a door is 128), turn it, stand it up (a model made with Z up comes lying down), make it two-sided (leaves, cloth, thin panels). Import: Deus Ex's own importer makes it a model of DeusEdix's package DeusEdixModels (in the game at once when it is closed, else with Install), and it is ready to place. Up to 30000 triangles and 8 textures (made 256 colours, as the game's); transparent parts of a picture become see-through (masked). Your models are listed at the bottom of the Import window, where they can be deleted.

Animated models. A glTF/GLB, FBX or DAE with a skeleton (or shape keys) and animations keeps them. Deus Ex's models have no bones: every animation of the game is a series of frames of the model's points. The Import window lists the file's animations: tick the ones to keep and give each a name (the game's own names, such as Walk, Run or BreatheLight, are offered), choose how many frames a second they are kept at (15 by default; more is smoother and heavier) and which one the model loops in the map. Each animation is played and recorded frame by frame into the model, as a sequence of its own. A placed model plays its loop in the game (IdleAnim in its Properties, IdleRate for its speed); in the editor it shows its first frame. At most 4000 frames, and 3 million frames x points per model: for a long animation, fewer frames a second or a simpler model. A clip that walks away from its place (root motion) walks away in the game too: export it "in place" (Mixamo has the option). Some files cannot be used as they are: an FBX the browser's FBX reader cannot open (one with very many shape keys runs it out of memory) or one whose skeleton does not fit its model as read (a scaled armature, two skeletons: the Import window says so and leaves the animations out). Saving the model as glTF/GLB from Blender, with the armature's scale applied, solves both. A flat model (a rug, a panel) takes the height you give as the size of its longest side.

Simplify. Today's models often have 100 000 triangles or more; Deus Ex's own characters about 1000. When a model is too detailed for the game (more than 30000 triangles or 8191 points) the Import window says so and offers Simplify: give the triangles to keep (3000 by default; 2000-3000 suits a character, a few thousand a big object) and the model is made lighter where its shape changes least, its textures' layout counted too. It keeps a part of the model's own points, so the textures, the skeleton, the shape keys and the animations stay as they were. It also works on a model that is not too detailed, to make it lighter; Original goes back to it as it was read. (The simplifier is meshoptimizer, MIT licence, Arseny Kapoulkine.) A model made of thousands of separate small pieces, such as tufts of fur, keeps more points for its triangles.

Zones: water, rooms of their own, rain and snow, the sky

Deus Ex divides a map into zones: closed areas, each with its own light, sound, fog, gravity, damage, water. The editor makes them the way the game does.

  • + Water (bottom bar): click a floor: a pool 256 x 256, 96 deep, cut into it. Its surface is a zone portal, so the water is a zone of its own: JC swims and dives, the view turns blue under water. In its panel: the liquid (clean water, dirty water, toxic water that hurts with radiation, a burning liquid), where the surface is, a place name; untick cut a pool to fill a basin the map already has. Resize it with R or the numbers.
  • + Portal: click the floor in a doorway: an invisible sheet fills it exactly (it finds the walls and the top by itself). Put one in every opening of a room: the room becomes a zone of its own.
  • Objects > Zones: put one inside the closed area. Zone: its place name, ambient light and colour, sound of the zone (heard everywhere in it), hurts (damage per second, kind of damage: radiation, burned, shocked, poison gas...; a message), instant death, own gravity, ground friction (0.2 = ice). Rain zone and Snow zone (Revision's): rain or snow falls round JC in the whole zone. Water zone, Sky zone for those who build them by hand.
  • Reverb (a zone's Properties, as Unity's Audio Reverb Zone presets): how the zone echoes the sounds made in it. None, Small room, Office, Hall, Tunnel / Sewer, Hangar or Cave set it in one step (one Ctrl+Z): the zone's reverb as Unreal Engine 1 has it, the echo on, how loud the echoes are together, a cutoff (the echoes lose the sounds above it: bare walls give them back, carpet and rock swallow them) and six echoes, each when it comes back (milliseconds) and how loud (0-255). Hall is the game's own (NSF headquarters, the MJ12 lab). Custom turns the echo on with the values as they are; Values (under the row, open by itself for Custom) shows them, a preset's too: change any of them. The original game's Galaxy audio plays these echoes. In Revision Galaxy plays them too and its OpenAL audio imitates them; a RevZoneInfo (and Revision's sound zone, water zone, rain and snow zones) also has an OpenAL reverb, one of OpenAL's EFX reverbs (cave, hangar, sewer pipe and over a hundred more) that its OpenAL audio uses instead: the presets set it with the echoes' delays, loudness and cutoff. A water zone keeps its own (underwater): the presets leave it, None puts it back. The echoes' gains stay the class's there (150 and 70, then none): such a zone's own Gain is OpenAL's and hides them, so its Values have no echo gains (one such a zone still has, from an older editor, is left out at Rebuild, and Messages says so). Rebuild puts the reverb in the map; the editor does not play it (only the game tells how it sounds).
  • The sky: a hole in a ceiling (+ Hole, click the ceiling) with open to the sky ticked shows the sky through it. A map with no sky of its own gets a sky box (Hong Kong at night, New York at night, stars, sunset, clouds, bright clouds, orange haze: choose it in the hole's panel).
  • Map > Sky & time of day: Day, Sunset or Night. It paints a sky of your own (its colours or stars), or uses a picture of yours or one of the game's skies, and sets the light: a sun shining in through every hole open to the sky, on the side of the sun, and the ambient light of the map. Deus Ex has no sun of its own: the sun is made of lights. Rebuild puts it in the game.
  • Clouds (same window), over any of those skies: None, Soft, Puffy, Streaks (long wisps along the wind) or Game's (the game's own cloud textures: the Hong Kong cloud bank, the wide one, the fluffy blue ones, the subtle one), and ↻ for other painted clouds of the same kind. Amount and Brightness (painted clouds), Size, Height (how high the layer is: its far edge, in degrees over the horizon; low clouds reach down to it), Wind (how fast they move; 0 = still) and Wind from (the direction, measured like the sun's). The preview shows them as the game will, moving: drag it to look round and up. They are made the way the game's own levels make them (Wan Chai Street): a sheet in the sky box whose texture the engine moves (Auto U Pan, at the speed the sky zone gives), so they work the same in the original Deus Ex and in Revision. The painted sky keeps the far clouds under the layer's edge. The clouds go in the sky box the editor makes, so a map that has a sky of its own (most of the game's maps) keeps its own clouds.

After Rebuild, Messages say if a zone is not closed (it leaks into the zone round it, often the whole map: its water, damage or fog would not be where you meant) or if the start of the map is inside a zone that does something to JC at once (water, hurts, instant death, own gravity); one that only echoes, sounds or lights says nothing.

Packages of other mods

Packages... (bottom bar, next to Characters...) lists the packages (.u) in the game's folders that the editor does not load yet (Deus Ex\System, Revision\System, Revision\HDTP\System), and Another .u file takes one kept elsewhere. Tick them and Apply: the page loads again and their actors are in the palette. Decorations, characters, weapons and items with a 3D model go to their usual group, with the real model. Triggers, zones and other logic go under the package's name. They have their Properties and Interactions, and are placed, saved and rebuilt like the game's own.

A map that uses them needs the package in the game too: a package kept outside the game's folders must also be copied into Deus Ex\System (or Revision\System). Packages with only textures, sounds or models (no actor classes) add nothing to the palette.

Scripts and the Console

Tools > Scripts... writes UnrealScript inside the editor. A script is an invisible object placed in the map (it extends UCScript): Started runs when the map starts, Triggered when a rule switches it on (Untriggered when it switches it off), Repeat every few seconds. Helpers do the common things: the player, say a line, find an object, read and set flags, give an item. Compile builds the scripts' own package in a few seconds and lists the problems by line: click one to go there. Place in map puts the script in the level and asks at once what sets it off: click a button, a trigger, a timer... (or Esc, and connect it later like any object).

A script as a block. A script can be a block the rules use like a door or a keypad: its inputs are what a rule can make it do, its outputs what it tells the rules. They are declared at the end of the script, in its defaultproperties:

Inputs(0)=(Id="Start",Label="Start it",Args="Seconds")
Outputs(0)=(Id="Done",Label="it is done")

and written in Input(InputName, Args, Caller) (a switch on the input's name; ArgFloat(Args, "Seconds") reads an argument) and with Output("Done"). Then the Interactions offer Start it as something to do to the script (with a field for each argument), and it says "it is done" as something that can happen to it. The template A block of + New has both. A var() of the script is set per object in the Inspector.

The Console (a tab next to the Browser, or Window > Console) shows the game's log while the game runs, as it is written: Logic shows what the rules and the scripts do, in words (▶ L3 When the player uses Button1: open Door3, → open Door3, ⏸ L4 did not run: only if JC carries Multitool, and that does not hold); a name selects that object in the map, a rule's id goes to its object. Scripts shows only the lines from scripts (log()) and from the Console, Problems only errors and warnings. As Unity's Console, Collapse shows identical lines as one, with how many came at its right (×240: a script warning written at every frame is one line), and Time puts before each line the seconds since Play started (12.4, 1:05.0; the lines read back from an earlier log have none). Both stay as you leave them, and during Play the Console follows the log even while it is hidden, so that each line has its time. Copy puts lines on the clipboard as they read (with their time and count when shown): the lines picked (click a line, Ctrl+click one more, Shift+click those in between) or, with none picked, every line shown (the filter and Find in log choose them). In the log, Ctrl+C copies them too (a text selected with the mouse: that text), Ctrl+A picks every line shown, Up / Down the line before or after (Shift: one more) and Esc lets them go: these keys stay the Console's, the map's objects are not copied. The name of an object of the open map in any line is a link that selects it, as in the logic lines (ScriptWarning: DeusExMover 01_Lab.DeusExMover3 (Function ...) Accessed None); a name written with another map's name (Play went on to that map) is not one of this map's. During Play, the line at the bottom sends a command to the game's own console (summon, set, a cheat...). The game runs a cheat (god, summon, fly...) only with its cheats on: the first one you type turns them on for that game, and the Console says so.

Mods: export, install, play

When a campaign is finished, File > Export mod... saves it as one file (.dxmod) in Documents\DeusEdix Mods: its maps, its packages, a picture (the view you frame in the 3D view), its name, author and description, and the map it starts on. The editor refuses maps that need something the game does not have.

In the Projects window, Mods lists the mods installed in both games:

  • Install mod..., or drop a .dxmod (or a downloaded mod's .zip) on the window. Classic mods for Deus Ex or Revision are installed from their zip too (maps and packages; programs inside them are not run nor copied; a mod that replaces the game's own packages needs its own installer).
  • Play starts the game on the mod's first map, Starts on chooses another, Remove takes it away. A mod lives in its own folder (DeusEdixMods): the game's settings and other mods are not changed.

Map settings

Map in the top bar:

  • Music: Revision plays a track for every moment of the map: exploring (and the intro before it), combat, conversations, leaving the map, death. Same as the map takes the whole set of a map of the game; each moment can also be chosen on its own among every track of the game, and ▶ plays it. (The PS2 soundtrack option of Revision gets the same tracks, or the copied map's own PS2 set.)
  • Information: the name of the place (Place: the save list, the DataVault), the author, and up to four opening lines written on the screen when JC arrives ("Liberty Island, New York").
  • Ambient light: the light everything has where no lamp reaches, and its colour (Deus Ex maps use 0-20; maps with zones have their own in each zone).

Apply, then Rebuild. In the map they are its DeusExLevelInfo, its LevelInfo and a music actor of the editor's own (UCMusicInfo, a DXOggMusicInfo of Revision).

Player characters per map

Create and save a human character in Characters..., then choose Use as player to use its appearance in the open map. Alternatively, open Map settings > Player character and select it under This map. Apply, Save, then Rebuild. The Player Start inspector has a Change shortcut to the same setting; Player Start still chooses only where and in which direction the player appears.

When the player uses it works with every supported player character. Older versions called this event When JC uses it; existing rules keep working without being recreated. It refers to the controlled player, while a character has used it is the separate event for NPC orders.

For a campaign-wide choice, expand Project default, choose a character and press Set default. Rebuild each affected map. A map may inherit that default or override it with another character (including JC Denton). Thus map A can use one protagonist, map B another, and returning to A restores its chosen appearance. Map overrides use the map's normal Undo/Redo and Save; the project default is saved separately.

This first version changes the third-person body and eight skin slots only. Inventory, health, credits, skills, augmentations and story flags stay shared. First-person weapon hands, player voice, dialogue identity and mission scripts remain those of the game's normal player; changing appearance does not rewrite the campaign. Separate protagonist inventories are not available yet. All 17 human body templates in Character Creator are supported, including the different builds, uniforms, dresses and smaller bodies. Their gameplay collision and eye height remain those of the normal player: this is an appearance change, not a different-sized player or a different playable species. Imported arbitrary meshes and non-human bodies are not enabled by this feature.

Rebuild automatically adapts missing crouch-walk, weapon and other player motions from the installed game's player animations, including one-frame NPC placeholders. Existing usable animations, original frames and textures are preserved; the added motions are retargeted approximations, not newly authored bespoke animations. Footstep callbacks are retained. The copied body is stored in the private map, never over the game's original package. Preparation is cached; the first build of a body can take a few extra seconds. No game meshes or animation frames are bundled with DeusEdix.

Each configured map exposes an automatic Player is ... condition to the story tools (UCEdPlayer_<character-id>; hyphens become underscores). Only the current identity's flag is true. The choice survives normal map travel and native Save/Load in Original and Revision. Leaving configured project maps restores the original player's appearance. The editor prevents deletion of characters used by a project default or saved map; select a replacement first. A missing or unsupported character stops Rebuild with an explanation. Ordinary maps which have never enabled player characters stay unchanged.

Sounds

Objects > Sounds > Ambient sound: click where it should be heard (rain on a window, the hum of a machine room, wind on a roof, a crowd), then Choose... its sound in its Properties: the list has every sound of the game, the loops for places first; search by word (rain, hum, wind, water) and ▶ listens to it. Heard within is how far (the sphere in the 3D view: drag its dots), Volume, Pitch. Objects (a TV, a fridge, a generator) and lamps (the buzz of a neon) have the same Sound in their Properties.

Sound effects (once, when something happens), in Objects > Sounds:

  • Sound effect: when JC walks in: a zone (the purple cylinder, Radius and Height in its Properties): it plays when JC enters it. Only once, or again every time (Again after a few seconds).
  • Sound effect: when triggered: it plays when something triggers it. It gets a Tag of its own (SoundFX1...): put that Tag in the Event of a door (it plays when the door has finished opening), a button, a keypad (right code), a Trigger (Objects > Sounds > Trigger: a zone JC walks into), or use it from the Story. The Connections show the arrow.
  • After placing one, the sound list opens: choose its sound. In its Properties: Heard in JC's head (a radio, a voice: as loud wherever he is) or heard from its place within Heard within (the circles in the 3D view), Volume, Pitch. A sound effect that walks-in also triggers its own Event, like any trigger.
  • More sounds (a playlist, like Unity's Audio Random Container): so a dog, a drip or a distant siren does not sound the same every time. Drop sounds from the browser on its last row (or + Add...), up to 8 with its Sound; each has ▶, ↑ ↓ (its place in the list) and ✕, and a sound dropped on one replaces it. Each time it plays it picks one: Order Random (never the same one twice in a row) or In sequence. Pitch ± and Volume ± vary each play by up to that much. Repeats every a to b seconds: once it has played, it plays again by itself after a random wait between them, a zone while JC stays in it, a triggered one until an interaction does Stop its repeats on it (or something untriggers it); walking in or a trigger again meanwhile does not play it sooner; Only once never repeats. ▶ Preview plays four picks the way the game would (again: stop). A sound effect with one sound and none of these plays as it always did.

Doors have their sounds in their Properties (Door sounds): when it starts opening, when it is open, when it starts closing, when it is shut, and over and over while it moves; Heard within and Volume. The doors of the game have theirs already; a door copied from another map keeps them.

Hear the map

Hear the map (the speaker at the end of the 3D view's toolbar, or View > Hear the map), as Unity's Scene view audio: the map's ambient sounds play in the editor where they are, heard from the camera as you fly through the map, to balance their reach and loudness by ear before a Rebuild. It is off at first and kept for the next time (in the browser). While it is on:

  • Every object with a Sound of its own loops it: ambient sounds, lamps (the buzz of a neon), decorations (a TV, a generator), and the objects whose kind has one in the game (a security camera's hum, a vending machine's). Each one is heard as the game does: all of it at its place, fading in a straight line to nothing at its Heard within (25 x (radius + 1) units: the sphere in the view), at its Volume (the game plays ambient sounds at 0.7 of it) and Pitch (64: as recorded). A change in its Properties, or a move, is heard at once.
  • A zone's Sound of the zone is heard while the camera is in that zone, from the zone's object up to 6400 units, as the game does while JC's head is in it; out of its zone it is silent. Which zone the camera is in comes from the last Rebuild: before one (or in a map read by an older version of the editor) a zone's sound is heard within its own Heard within.
  • The 16 nearest sounds play at once, each from its side.
  • It is silent while Play runs, while the editor's tab is hidden and with no map open. The browser lets a page play sound only after a click or a key: opened with Hear the map on, the editor starts it at your first click.
  • The sounds come from the game as ▶ gets them for a preview: the first time a sound takes a moment (one at a time, the nearest first; none while a Rebuild runs). A sound the browser cannot play is said once in Messages.

Sounds in the browser, and your own

Sounds and My sounds use tiles like the object browser. Click a tile to select it and inspect its duration, source, format, channels and sample rate. Play draws the real waveform and playback position in both the tile and Inspector. The Inspector also offers seeking, preview volume and loop preview; these controls do not change the sound in the game. Find in map there shows the objects of the map that use the sound (the Hierarchy's search snd=, see Searching the map). Select a scene object to return to its properties. Then drag a sound:

  • onto the 3D view: a sound effect there, that plays when JC walks into its zone and is heard within its radius (both in its Properties, with the circles in the view);
  • onto a door or another object with sounds of its own: a menu asks which one (a door's opening, closing, moving sound; the hum of a lamp or a machine);
  • onto a sound in the Inspector: it replaces that one.

Your own sounds: Import... (top of the browser), or drop the files anywhere on the editor: WAV, MP3, OGG, FLAC. They are made mono (a sound heard from a place is mono in the game), 16 bit, 44.1 kHz, and become sounds of the editor's own package (UCSounds in DeusEdix, DeusEdixSounds in DeusEdix), which the game plays like its own; it is installed with your maps. Up to 10 minutes each. Importing the same name creates a numbered copy and keeps the previous sound. Drop one audio file on an object's sound field to import and assign it directly. Other file drops import into the browser, with a visible drop hint. A cancelled asset drag never leaves placement active. Download WAV in the imported sound's Inspector exports a usable file; dragging a prepared imported tile to the desktop also works in browsers that support file drag-out. Delete sound… in its Inspector removes it after confirmation (maps that use it lose it).

Check map

Check in the top bar lists what is wrong or missing, with go to:

  • errors: no Player start, a map exit to a map that does not exist, a character that patrols without a route, an object or a light inside a wall or a floor (as last rebuilt: stuck, invisible, or lighting nothing);
  • warnings: an event (or a keypad's, a security computer's) that triggers nothing, a patrol point leading to no point, patrol points with the same Tag, no light in the map, characters but no AI paths;
  • notes: a door whose key is not in this map (JC must bring it), unsaved changes.

It also looks at where things are, as Unity's Search finds objects overlapping others:

  • the same object twice at one spot: two things of the same class within 2 units of each other, turned the same way and with the same properties (All properties too), probably a double paste (Ctrl+D, Ctrl+V, an Alt+drag let go where it began). In the game they are both there: two pickups, two lights, a door twice. delete the extra one deletes the copy added last (the one there first stays). Two sounds, triggers or lights put at one spot on purpose are set differently (another sound, Event, radius...), and are left alone;
  • a decoration or a thing to pick up left in the air: its bottom more than 8 units above what is under it (the floor, a table, a block, another object: a ray straight down from its middle, as Drop to floor casts). What the 3D view hides (Isolate, the Show menu, an eye) counts too: a bottle on a table hidden in the view is on that table. The game drops these when the map starts. drop to floor does Drop to floor on it, onto what is under it whether the view shows it or not. Left out by what they are: the game's classes made to hang, to sit on a wall or to stay where they are put (their own Physics is not Falling in the game's classes: lamps on chains and hanging decorations, light bulbs, switches and buttons, taps and showers, fans, turrets, cameras and keypads, wall computers and ATMs, trees and outdoor things, vehicles, statues, Revision's ceiling lights and towel racks, and the editor's imported models), and anything whose Physics is set to something other than Falling (Stick to surface sets None on what it sticks to a wall) or whose bCollideWorld is False: set one of these on a thing that hangs on purpose and Check map leaves it alone;
  • a path node linked to no other point (as the last Rebuild made the AI paths): no character goes there. Move it within reach of another one, on the floor with nothing in between, and Rebuild.

Each fix is one Ctrl+Z, and the list is checked again after it. The warnings of the Build report in Messages (after a Rebuild: an object inside a wall, a zone that leaks...) end with a Check map link that opens it.

What you added or changed is checked fully; the original objects of a map of the game only for what breaks the map (they have links the story and the game's scripts use): two of the map's own objects at one spot, or one of them in the air, are as the game has them.

AI paths and patrols

The characters of Deus Ex walk on a network of path nodes: they go from node to node along the links the editor of the game works out from them (UnrealEd's Paths Define). The map editor makes that network at every Rebuild.

  • AI paths in the top bar (or P) shows the network as the last rebuild made it: green lines are links both ways, yellow lines one way only. A place without lines is a place characters cannot find their way to (they can still walk there in a straight line when they see it).
  • To add paths: Objects > AI > Path node, click the floor where characters must be able to walk (along corridors, through doors, round corners: one every 300-500 units, each in sight of the next), then Rebuild. The other points of the same group: Patrol point, Wander point (wandering characters stop there and look at its Look at Tag), Hide point (where scared characters hide).
  • The points rest on the floor by themselves: Deus Ex makes no link from a point floating higher than about 20 units, so the rebuild puts every path point of your maps 15 units above the floor below it, as UnrealEd does. The original maps have their points higher (up to about 40 units): for the links the rebuild rests them on the floor too, then puts each back where it was. The links of an original map that the rebuild does not make again (Battery Park's subway stairs) are kept, as long as both their points are still where they were and nothing has been built across them.

Navigation (the route icon in the 3D view's toolbar, next to the icons button): the two layers of navigation, each shown or hidden by itself, ticked in its menu: AI paths (the nodes' network, P) and Walkable area (the navigation mesh), one, the other or both; then Test route and Fill AI paths. The same are in the View and Map menus.

Test route (the Navigation menu, or View > Test a route): click where a character starts, then where it must go. With the AI paths shown, the editor draws the way the game's AI would take, node to node, with its length; if no path node is near one of the two places it says so. With the walkable area alone, it draws the straight way on it. Click again for another test, Esc to close.

Walkable area (the Navigation menu, or View > Walkable area) shows where a man can walk in the map: the editor makes a navigation mesh of the map's floors (with Recast, the library many games use for this, which comes with the editor; it takes a second or two) and colours the floor JC can reach from where he starts:

  • green: near the AI paths, characters find their way there;
  • amber: walkable, but far from any path node: put one there if characters must go;
  • red: no path node reaches it: characters cannot find their way there.

Only the main network counts: path points the last rebuild left linked among themselves but not to the rest (a group apart) do not make their floor green, and the status bar says how many there are. Roofs and places JC cannot reach are not coloured; the status bar says how big they are. With the walkable area shown, Test route also draws the straight way on it (in the accent colour) next to the AI's node-to-node one, with its length: where the two are very different, the network makes the characters walk round. The walkable area knows the map's walls and floors, not the furniture and the other objects, so it can show a way through a crate or a barricade.

Fill AI paths (Map > Fill AI paths) puts the path nodes for you: one wherever JC can walk and no node of the network is near enough, about 450 units apart, each where a man can walk straight to the next one, away from the walls and outside the furniture. Where a group of points does not reach the main network, it adds nodes along the way between them (at the corners). They are ordinary path nodes, added as if placed by hand: Ctrl+Z takes them all away, and the next Rebuild (or Map > Rebuild AI paths) links them; the walkable area counts them at once. After the rebuild, run it again: it joins what the game left apart, and says which groups it cannot join (steep stairs or a ledge the game does not link): put a few nodes there by hand, closer together. On Battery Park it adds about 170 nodes and the floor goes from 23% to 97% green.

Patrol route: select a character; at the end of its Properties, Draw route, then click the floor where they walk, point after point, and press Enter (or Esc). They walk it in a loop: after the last point, back to the first. The route is drawn in orange with numbers; the dashed line is where they start from. Every point has a wait (seconds they stop there, turned the way the point faces: turn it with E) and ✕ takes it out of the route. + Add points goes on drawing, Clear route removes it. The character's Orders become Patrolling and its Order target the first point; each patrol point's Next point is the following one (all of this is also in their Properties, and in Connections).

Sitting and getting up

Use a chair, bench or sofa from Objects: these have the game's seat points. A chair made only of Pieces or Prefabs is geometry and cannot seat an NPC by itself.

In Interactions, choose Sit on a chair, then Pick… and click the seat in the scene or Hierarchy. The character walks to that specific seat and uses the game's sitting animations. Stand up gets them up again. You can trigger either action from a button, a proximity rule, or another event. The Story also offers them under A character…, including actions after a dialogue. A rule When the map starts can send a character to their chair at startup.

Keep the front of the chair clear and connect distant seats with AI paths. Occupied or unavailable seats are rejected; the character does not choose another chair instead. The body must support the game's sitting animations. Commands received during a conversation wait until it ends. The original Behaviour > Orders > Sitting remains available: its target is the chair's engine Tag, or the game chooses a seat if no target is set.

Animations

What a character does when idle: in its Properties, Animation > When idle lists the animations of its model (breathing, idle gestures, sitting, cowering, dancing...) with their length; ▶ plays it on the character in the 3D view. In the game they do it while standing (Orders Standing) or while waiting at a patrol point, instead of breathing; Speed plays it slower or faster. In a conversation, in a fight or running away they are as the game makes them.

At a patrol point: select the point, Animation while waiting (the animations of the characters patrolling through it): they do it while they wait there. When the point had no wait, it gets one long enough for the animation.

When something happens (an Interaction): Play a body animation on a character makes it play one when the player uses a button, walks into a collision box, picks something up, when a timer runs out, a cutscene starts... Choose the character as the target, then its animation (the model's own, or one of yours from the Animation Studio: it is put on the character's models when you choose it), how many times (once, over and over until Stop its body animation, or over and over, for some seconds), its speed, and whether it may keep walking (off: it stands still while it plays; a patrol goes on afterwards). ▶ plays it on the character in the 3D view. Whatever the game's AI plays meanwhile, the animation goes on where it was; at its end the character does what it did before, and the rules When it has finished its body animation run (to play another, say a line, open a door...).

All of these work through DeusEdix's package: UCAnimSet objects in the map (made by the rebuild), so the package must be in the game (Install puts it there).

Pose here (Animation > Pose here... in a character's Properties, or right-click it): poses the character where it stands, against the map around it, instead of in the Studio's empty room: a hand on a railing or a desk, the back against a wall. The character shows its skeleton's handles: drag the hands (orange) and the feet (blue) onto the map and they stick to what is under the pointer (a hand lies on it, the palm against it, the fingers pointing on away from the shoulder; a foot stands on it); Alt moves them freely. A handle that sticks stays where it touches, and grabbing it again moves nothing until the pointer moves. A thin railing is easy to miss by a pixel: dragged near it, the hand gets onto it, and slipping just past its edge does not drop the hand onto the floor behind it. The hips (green) move the body (Shift: up and down) and, with Ctrl, the whole character moves in the map to come closer to what it leans on, the hands that hold on staying where they are. A handle turns red when the arm or the leg cannot reach. A hand that sticks closes by itself as on what it holds: round a railing or an edge (the surface ends just past the fingers), relaxed on a table top; the Grip sliders override it. Start from gives a ready pose, placed on what you click next: Railing (both hands on it), Elbows (leaning on it), Wall (the back against it), Table (both hands on the top) and Sit (on an edge, a crate, a bench, facing the camera): the character turns, comes close enough for its arms to reach (never into what it holds; it leans more when it cannot come closer) and puts its hands and feet down; a seat too high to sit on with the feet on the floor is said. The panel leans, turns and bends the upper body, closes the hands (Grip) and keeps the character breathing. Save makes it an animation of the Animation Studio (Pose<name>), puts it on the character's models and gives it to the character as what it does when idle, with Stays put (it never turns away from what it leans on); Rebuild puts it in the map. Opening Pose here again on that character goes back to the same pose. Animate in the Studio... saves the pose as a new animation with the name you give it, without changing the character, and opens it in the Animation Studio: the pose is its first key, to go on animating from there. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (or the arrows in the panel) step back and forth through the changes made while posing, the character's place in the map included; Reset (standing again) is one of them, so a mistake never means starting over. Esc or Cancel leaves without changing anything.

Animation Studio

Animations in the top bar (or Animation Studio... in a character's Properties) opens the Studio, to make new animations for the characters, change the game's, or bring in animations from other programs.

Deus Ex animates its characters frame by frame, without a skeleton. The Studio finds a skeleton in every model by itself, from the game's own animations: the parts of the body that always move together are a bone, the points where two of them turn are the joints. It takes a moment the first time a model is opened; Make the skeleton again redoes it.

Every human gets the same skeleton of 17 bones. The joints are placed where a body has them (shoulders, elbows, hips, knees and ankles at their usual share of the height) and then fitted to the model's own motion, so a long coat or a skirt does not move a knee; the knee sits on the line from the hip to the ankle, a little forward. Children are the same skeleton, smaller.

The game's characters never bend their wrists, so the hands are found from the model itself: the vertices of the lower arm with the skin's texture (the head's) are the hand, and the wrist is where the sleeve ends; with gloves or bare arms, what lies past the wrist. The animations made before the hands had bones (18 October 2026) keep the skeleton they were made with, so they look the same.

  • Character and Model: the character to work on, and its model. A soldier of the game can have two: the original and the HDTP one (Revision's high definition models). The Studio shows the one the game shows and puts every animation on both.
  • Game animations: click one to play it. Copy makes it an animation of yours, to change.
  • + New: an animation of your own, starting from the rest pose. Click a ball of the skeleton (or the body) to pick a bone and turn it with the rings; the hips also move (G or W move, R or E turn). Every change is a key at that frame (the yellow diamonds on the timeline); between keys the Studio moves smoothly. Go to another frame (the slider, the arrow keys), pose again. Space plays. Delete removes the key of the frame, Mirror swaps left and right, Reset bone puts a bone back, From game ... Pose copies the pose of a frame of a game animation. Frames and fps give its length, loops whether it repeats.
  • Import file: an animation made elsewhere, on any human skeleton: BVH (motion capture: free libraries such as CMU's), FBX (for example from Mixamo: download it in FBX, with or without skin) or glTF/GLB (Blender exports it). The Studio recognises the bones by their usual names (Hips, Spine, LeftArm, LeftForeArm, LeftUpLeg, upperarm_l, thigh_l...) and moves each bone of the Deus Ex skeleton the way the file's does, whatever the file's rest pose (a T-pose too) and size. It stays in place: walking forward is the game's job. The bones the file does not have follow their parent (they are listed). A file on the Studio's own skeleton (see Export .glb) comes back as it was made: the body's motion and the frame rate too.
  • IK (the buttons L hand, R hand, L foot, R foot under the frame's title): an arm or a leg moved by its hand or foot. A box at the hand or foot is the controller: G moves it and the limb follows, R turns the hand or foot; the diamond (the pole) is where the elbow or the knee points. The controller has keys of its own (its row in the list, Key controller), and a foot on IK stays where it is while the body moves. Clicking the button again goes back to turning the bones one by one (FK): the bones get keys holding the IK's poses.
  • Grip (in the panel when a hand, or an arm's IK, is selected): how far the hand is closed, from 0 (open) to 100. It is keyed on its own row (Left grip, Right grip) like a bone. The game's models have no fingers of their own, so the closing is the game's: the way the model's own animations close its hands (holding a rifle), so it looks as in the game. The HDTP models never close their hands in their animations: their fingers bend at the knuckles instead. Without grip keys the hands are as before.
  • Export .glb: the character with its skeleton, its weights, its textures and the animation open, for Blender or any other program that reads glTF. In Blender set the scene to the animation's frame rate (the Studio says it), then File > Import > glTF 2.0. Animate there, export glTF again and Import file here.
  • Graph editor: pick a bone and click its curves (or Edit curves) to adjust its X/Y/Z rotations in degrees. Ctrl+Space or the maximize button fills the Studio with it, and again gives the view back; it fits the curves when it opens. Body position edits the character's position (X forward, Y up, Z right). Opening the graph does not change an animation. Enabling curves retains its poses at every game frame, including imported motion; constant axes start with one key. The graph and the game bake use the same curves. Click or Shift-click keys, drag to change their frame/value, or type precise values. Smooth rounds the movement without overshooting the neighboring values, Linear maintains a constant speed, and Hold retains a value until the next key. Interpolation applies after the selected key. Add a key at the playhead, duplicate selected keys there, or delete them; collisions with other keys are rejected. Fit all/selected, mouse-wheel zoom and middle-button drag help navigate. Graph keys affect their selected channel; the yellow timeline keys remain whole poses.
  • Numbers: hold the label of a number (X, Y, Z, Scale %, Frames, fps) and drag left or right; a faster mouse makes bigger steps, Shift is fine, Esc puts it back. One drag is one undo step.
  • Keys as in Blender: G move, R rotate, S scale (props), Alt+G / Alt+R / Alt+S back to rest; I keys the bone (Shift+I the pose), Alt+I or X deletes the key; Up / Down the next and previous key, Shift+Left / Right the first and last frame; Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V copy and paste a pose (Ctrl+Shift+V mirrored); F or Numpad . looks at the selection; Numpad 1 / 3 / 7 front, right and top (Ctrl: the other side). In the Graph: A all keys, Alt+A none, G moves the keys with the mouse (a click or Enter places them, Esc or a right click puts them back), X delete, Shift+D duplicate, I a key at the playhead, T easing, Home fit, F fit the selected keys.
  • Undo / Redo: the Studio keeps its own history, including posing, curves and animation settings. Use the graph buttons or Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z / Ctrl+Y. One drag is one undo step.
  • Save puts it in the game: it becomes frames of the model, like the game's own, for every model of the character (original and HDTP), in DeusEdix's package DeusEdixAnims.u, installed in the game when it is closed.

To use it: a character's Properties, When idle, the group Animation Studio (yours). Every human character can use it: the Studio puts it on that character's models when you choose it (a few seconds). In the game the character gets a copy of its model with your animations added (all the game's are still there), whatever model the game gave it (HDTP or not).

Animations are files in tools\mapkit\anims\ (<name>.json: the keys of the bones), the skeletons in anims\rigs\, the frames made for each model in anims\baked\.

Props attached to bones

In Animation Studio, open Props and choose + Add prop. Search the game's objects (for example Liquor40oz for a bottle), select one and attach it. The selected bone is used; the default hips selection attaches to Right hand. Bone can move the attachment to any other bone.

  • Click the prop in the view to select it (a yellow box): the gizmo moves (G), turns (R) and scales (S) it in every direction; it stays in its bone's frame. Alt+G / Alt+R / Alt+S put its offset, turn or size back.
  • Offset and Rotation fit the grip in bone-local axes (X forward, Y up, Z right at rest). Scale % changes the object's size. Snap to bone resets its offset and rotation.
  • Enabled includes or excludes it in both the preview and game; Remove removes this attachment only. All prop edits support the Studio's Undo/Redo and stay with the saved animation.
  • Pose the arm normally and scrub or play: the prop follows the bone. Save, assign the animation to the character, then Rebuild the map to include the attachments. Saving alone does not rebuild maps.

Props are visual accessories, not pickups: no collision, inventory or AI changes. They appear only while the character plays that animation, follow its speed and loops, and disappear when it changes animation or dies. Up to eight props per animation, on Original and HDTP character models. This first version has a fixed grip throughout the animation: no keyed pickup/drop or hand switching yet. The game's Cigarettes object is a packet, not a single cigarette.

New map

New map (top bar, next to the list of maps) makes a map of your own:

  • Name and Mission: the file becomes <mission>_<name>.dx (for example 06_Warehouse_9). The map belongs to that part of the campaign: the mission's goals, flags and InfoLinks work in it. Place name is what the game shows as the location (optional).
  • An empty room to build in: its size (width, depth, height; a door is 64 x 128) and the textures of floor, walls and ceiling (smooth concrete by default). It has lights, as bright and of the colour of the Mood you choose (Normal, Dark, Warm or Cold; in a Dark room some flicker: they blink at random in Revision, whose renderer never lights a flickering lamp), the start of the map and an arrival point called Start. It is a map script (tools\mapkit\maps\new_<map>.py) built at once: add Pieces, Objects, characters and blocks, then Rebuild.
  • A copy of a map: any map of the game or of yours under the new name, as a map of your own (maps\): change it as you like, the original stays as it is.
  • A map template of the project: its tile shows its picture and name, its description is under the tiles and in its tooltip. The new map is the template's base (the room of the map it was saved from, built at once, or a copy of that map) with what was made in it, under the new name, mission and place name; the pictures you imported are copied into the new map's own folder, and a map exit that led into the old map leads into the new one. × on a tile deletes the template (the maps made from it stay as they are).

File > Save map as template... (as Unity's Save As Scene Template) keeps the open map, as it is now (saved or not), as a template of the project: give it a name and a description; the 3D view as you see it, without the gizmo, outlines and icons, becomes its picture (frame a nice view first). It keeps the place: blocks, drawings, structures (pasted and the map's own, moved or deleted), objects, lights, the start, map exits, zones, sounds, the sky, the time of day and Map settings, folders, and the pictures they use. It leaves out what belongs to that map's story: the characters (with their tactics and animations), Interactions (and usable objects and scripting tags), cutscenes and their cameras, the texts of computers, books and data cubes, the Story itself, the Conversation name (BindName) of any object, the place name and the opening lines of Map settings; the walkable area is made again by the next Rebuild. Only maps made with New map (an empty room or a copy) and the project's map files can be templates: a map built by a script of its own cannot.

Install puts the new map in the game.

Links between maps

JC leaves a map through a map exit: he walks into it and the next map loads. He appears at an arrival point of that map, or at its start (its PlayerStart). Both are in Objects > Level (with Player start), or in the Links panel.

  • Place a Map exit where it makes sense (a doorway, the end of an alley, the top of a stair). In its Properties, Leads to lists every map; arriving at lists the arrival points of the map you chose (the game's own ones too), or the start of the map.
  • Place an Arrival point where JC should appear, turned the way he should look. Its Tag is its name: other maps lead to <this map>#<Tag>.
  • Links (top bar) shows the ways out of this map (where each exit leads), its arrival points, and every map of the game or of yours with an exit that leads here.
  • A dialogue or an event of the Story can also take JC to another map (action Take JC to another map).

To put a new map in the campaign: open the map of the campaign JC should come from (for example 06_HongKong_WanChai_Market; the editor makes your copy, the original stays as it is). Place a map exit there, leading to your map at Start. In your map, place a map exit back to that map, at one of its arrival points (the list shows the game's own) or at a new arrival point you place there. Rebuild both maps and Install. Play from here lets you try the way in. The exits are read from the built maps: after a Rebuild, Links and the lists show them.

Mission graph (read only)

The map icon at the top of the Inspector, to the right of its padlock, opens Mission graph in a separate analysis window. A pinned Inspector uses the object displayed there. The analyser reads saved maps, characters, flags, conversations and scripts in the active project: Original, Revision and UNATCO are analysed separately, without mixing their game variants.

First inspection

  1. Select Anna or another character and open Mission graph. The default view is Rules.
  2. Choose Mission and Map. The mission starts from the selected map's number; All missions also shows later appearances. Rules are grouped by mission and map. Map not resolved means the scanner has not established a map, not that the rule runs everywhere.
  3. Expand a rule card. When, Conditions and Actions describe the source block. Reads flags are states that it checks; Writes flags are states that it changes. These are not current values from a running save. Separate branches stay separate; conditions retain AND, OR and negation.
  4. Click a flag. Who writes it and Who checks it list the scanned rules across all missions. Open an entry to see that rule, even if it concerns another character. Back to Anna Navarre (or your starting selection) restores the original subject and mission filter.
  5. Details → Source statements & conditions shows the file, line and individual source spans. Notes explain actor loops, inherited script bindings, partial control flow and unresolved names.

For a first Anna exercise, choose Mission 03, map 03_NYC_UNATCOHQ, and find MS_AnnaDeskHome. The block checks AnnaAtDesk and MS_AnnaDeskHome, sets Anna's home position and marks the latter flag true. Click MS_AnnaDeskHome to compare its readers and writers. Original and Revision may both have source definitions in a Revision project; this does not prove that both execute.

Rules match the selected subject by explicit class, dialogue identity or actor reference. A flag whose name starts with that identity is separately labelled Matching flag name · not an actor reference. Unrelated flags from other branches of the same script are not automatically treated as this character's rules. A script may select several instances of a class; a conversation owner is an identity, not a guaranteed instance in the selected map.

Who writes it and Who checks it name scripts, conversations or authored rules, not necessarily the character performing the action. No writer found does not mean that a flag is unused: the engine, missing source code and computed flag names may add accesses that cannot be resolved statically. Conversation events and choices are separate records; their branch order is not simulated.

Connections in the view selector keeps the previous graph available: Dialogues, Behaviour, Story states and Other appearances open filterable reference lists. Select a reference to see one path, then click an arrow for its explanation or expand Direct connections to continue. Clear path clears that path. Find… searches project-wide; Back still restores your first object. Other appearances are separate instances with a shared class or identity, not proof of a unique character across maps. Shared scripts may also concern other characters. Arrows are references, not execution order. Long lists use Show more.

Findings and coverage

Findings lists issues on the current graph selection/path, with an expandable project list:

  • Error: a verifiable saved-reference problem, such as a rule targeting an actor deleted from a fully decoded map.
  • Review: a possible problem that needs checking, such as a destination outside scanned maps.
  • Unverified: missing sources, incomplete maps, computed names or runtime actor selection.

More → Coverage reports what was scanned. Rule cards reconstruct literal access and lexical if/else conditions, not full execution. Function calls, inherited dispatch, jumps, returns and switch branches may need source review; partial control flow is marked. Map guards are explicit when found; script bindings are candidate scopes, and numbered mission grouping follows the source/map naming. Authored rule cards retain saved conditions/actions, including choices, without simulating them.

The graph is not a live debugger, a narrative consistency checker or a visual script editor. It cannot prove that a rule runs, that branches conflict, or that changing a character is safe. No errors means neither complete coverage nor no risk.

Refreshing the analysis

Refresh reads saved project files again; reopening can reuse a cached scan. Unsaved changes are not included. More → Read game sources explicitly reads installed packages into local analysis caches when sources are missing or stale; progress stays visible. After an analysis-service update, restart DeusEdix before refreshing so that the new backend is loaded.

Scanning never saves or Rebuilds maps, compiles runtime code, changes dialogue, writes flags or alters a running game. Generated maps need an existing built snapshot; missing coverage is reported instead of silently building one. Esc closes the window. Inspecting a rule does not apply any change.

Story

Story (top bar), or Dialogues... on a selected character, opens the story of the map. It has three lists: dialogues, InfoLinks and events. Save keeps your work; Apply in game turns it into UnrealScript, compiles it into DeusEdix's package and installs it (the game must be closed; it takes a few seconds).

Everything is checked before it is applied: an empty line, an answer that leads nowhere or a missing character are listed under the panel.

Dialogues as a graph, and Play-test

A dialogue opens as a Graph (the List button shows the same steps in order). Each step is a node: a line in the colour of who speaks (JC blue, the character violet), JC's answers with an arrow from each one, conditions with Yes and No; the arrows show where the dialogue goes on.

  • Drag the dot under a step onto another step to connect them, or onto empty space to add the next step there (a menu offers every kind). Double-click empty space for a step on its own.
  • Double-click a line to write it in the node itself: Enter goes on to the next line, said by the other speaker; Shift+Enter starts a new row of text; Tab gives the line to another speaker; Esc is done. Double-click one of JC's answers to write it there too: Enter goes to the next answer (from the last one, a new answer). A click on the name in a line chooses who says it.
  • Every line shows the face of who says it: the game's own InfoLink portrait when the character has one (Alex Jacobson, Paul...), else a picture of the head of their model in the map, made by the editor.
  • Enter on a selected step adds the next line, said by the other speaker, ready to write. Delete removes a step: what led to it goes on to where it went. Ctrl+Z undoes. Fit shows the whole dialogue, Tidy lays it out again.
  • The selected step is edited at the side, with the same fields as in the List. Steps nothing reaches are faded; a step with a problem (an empty line, an answer that goes nowhere) is marked.

Play-test plays the dialogue in the editor as the game will: choose JC's answers, and the step it is at lights up. At the side, the state it reads (flags, credits, health, skill points, what JC carries): change it and the dialogue starts again with it. Answers the game would hide are greyed.

InfoLinks

An InfoLink is a radio call. It is one-way: only the caller speaks; JC never answers by radio (if JC has to report something, set a flag). Choose the caller among the portraits of the game (Alex Jacobson, Walton Simons, Jock, Paul Denton, Sam Carter, Jaime Reyes, Anna Navarre, Gunther Hermann, Tracer Tong, Bob Page, Morgan Everett, Gary Savage, Stanton Dowd, Daedalus, Icarus) or someone else with any name. Each line gets its own screen, timed like the game's.

An InfoLink never starts over a conversation or another InfoLink: it waits. As in Deus Ex, its text goes to the history and its actions happen at the start, so an InfoLink cut short counts as heard. Events and dialogues play it (action Play an InfoLink); + Event that plays it creates the event for you, and Plays when lists what plays it.

No talking while it plays (on unless you turn it off): while the call is on screen, clicking people starts no dialogue, because a dialogue would hide the InfoLink window and the game would cut the call. As soon as it ends, people can be talked to again. Turn it off for a call that may play while JC is busy with someone.

Events

An event happens when JC gets close to a point, when a flag changes, at map start, when a character dies or is knocked out, when a faction turns hostile to JC, or when JC has an item, once or every time, and only if its conditions are met. For the point, select an object in the scene and press Use the selected object, or look at the spot and press Use the point in front of the camera.

Flags

Flags are the story's memory: one dialogue sets them, another requires them. The flag field suggests the flags already in use, plus two kinds the story makes by itself: UCEd<code>_<id>_Played (a dialogue has been played) and UCEd<code>_<id>_Heard (an InfoLink has been heard); the InfoLink panel shows its own.

What happened to a character. DeusEdix remembers, for the whole game, every character JC killed or knocked out, in every map: no flag to set by hand. The 👤 button next to every flag field (Only if, If a flag..., events, Set a flag) picks it: the map (this one or any other), the character and was killed, was knocked out, was killed or knocked out or is alive. The field then reads, for example, 👤 TriadRedArrow0 (06_HongKong_WanChai_Street) was killed. Example: two dialogues for Simons in a later map, the first Only if that Red Arrow member was killed, the second with no condition: Simons brings it up only if JC killed him.

They are ordinary flags (UCFate<map code>_<name>_Dead, _KO, _Down), so a story can also set them itself with Set a flag: a character who "dies" in a cutscene counts as killed. The last value written wins. A knocked-out character that is killed later counts as killed; one carried to another spot keeps its fate.

Example: a guard gives JC a weapon, then a radio call

  1. Select the guard in the scene and press Dialogues...: a new dialogue opens with the guard as the speaker. Set Starts to when JC gets close.
  2. Write his line. Add JC's answers with three answers (GEP gun, crossbow, sniper rifle).
  3. Each answer created its own branch at the bottom: a label (answer1, answer2...), a line and End of dialogue. In each branch add Give JC an item with that weapon and move it up (↑) under the label.
  4. In each branch, before End of dialogue, add Actions with A character... (the guard) walks to a point, and pick the point.
  5. Create an InfoLink: caller Alex Jacobson, one or two lines.
  6. In the guard's dialogue, add to the Actions step Play an InfoLink (Alex's).
  7. Apply in game, open the game and walk to the guard.

Rebuild in the background

Rebuild (and Full rebuild, Rebuild lighting, Rebuild AI paths in the Map menu and the Rebuild button's arrow) no longer covers the editor while it works. A chip beside the status in the menu bar says the stage the build is at, the time so far and how long the last build of this map took (last time 0:41), with Cancel. The stages are the ones the build really has: Saving and Walkable area (the page, before the engine), then Preparing, Loading the map, Applying edits, CSG and BSP, AI paths, Lighting and Writing the .dx, read from the engine's own log as it works (a lighting-only build skips CSG and BSP, a build of objects only skips most of them). The build works out of sight: it opens no window on your screen, never takes the keyboard from the editor, and leaves alone the mouse of a game playing in its own window.

While it runs the 3D view is look-only: you can fly, orbit, select (in the view or the Hierarchy), frame with F, keep and recall views and selections (1…9), measure, switch the lighting and the AI paths, and read the Inspector. Editing waits until the build ends: every object is locked as by its padlock (no gizmo, the Inspector's fields greyed, the Hierarchy's drag and delete off), the asset browser is dimmed, and the keys that change the map (Delete, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+D, End, B...) only say so in the status bar; Tab still moves through the editor, and Enter or Space press the button that has the focus (the chip's Cancel too). What waits for an answer of the server (a texture applied, a sign's picture, the sky, Pose here's Save, Paste T3D) is refused while the build runs, and so is its answer when the build started while it was on its way, or when it comes while the map is being opened again after the build: the tool says so and nothing changes (make it again when the map is open). A change a tool window or a menu command would still make is taken back at once, and Messages says so, so the map stays the one being built (the build would not have it, and reloading the map after it would lose it). The AI assistant's changes wait too (it is told a Rebuild runs). Save, Play, View in game, Install, opening another map, closing this one and switching projects wait too, and a second Rebuild cannot start (the server refuses it, from another tab as well) until this one ends. If the map cannot be saved first, nothing is built, and Messages says so.

Cancel stops the build's own engine process (a map made by a script: the script and the engine it started), never anything else and never the game, and puts the map's last build back: the 3D view, the Lit view, Play and Install see the map exactly as before you pressed Rebuild. Your saved changes stay saved, ready for the next Rebuild. Pressed while the map is still being saved or its walkable area made, the build does not start at all. A build that compiles the project's player characters first (Map settings > Player characters) stops when its engine starts.

At the end Messages gets the build's lines and a Build report on top of them: the time of each stage (where the minute went: lighting or paths), what the engine counted (BSP nodes and surfaces and the zones it found, after CSG; the lights it lit with; the AI path points, the reach specs it made and pruned and the map's own links it put back), and the warnings (objects inside walls, zones that leak, stretched textures, edits skipped...), each with a Check map link that opens Check map on the map just built.

Play from here

Play from here opens the game on this map with JC standing on the floor below the camera, looking where you look, and then the controls are yours: walk around, talk, test a dialogue or an event. In its window (Play settings... in the Map menu and under the arrow between Rebuild and Play) you choose, besides a play scenario (below):

  • Mission state: the map as it starts.
  • Other flags: any flag, as Name=1 or Name=0, separated by commas.
  • Play in: Editor (the game plays inside the editor: see Play in the editor below) or Game window (the game's own window). The Play button plays in the editor; Play in editor and Play in game window are also in the Map menu and under the arrow.

Play first saves the map and its Story, then prepares a temporary copy of the map for the game, so the map you edit stays as it is: your latest dialogues are compiled into the project's package, the map is built with your changes and the packages are installed in the game, each only when something changed (see Play preparation below). On a test PC JC can move about 6 to 9 seconds after you press Play in Revision when the map has not changed (13.6 seconds in earlier versions of the editor), and about 6 seconds in the original game (34 seconds in earlier versions, which left DirectDraw on). Most of that time is the game's own start: Steam takes a second or two to start it and the game a few seconds to load the map, while the editor's part, before Steam starts the game, is under a second. A changed map adds its build.

For existing maps, Install keeps the previous copy in maps\backup\, refuses if your copy was changed by something else since your rebuild, and archives the changes, which are now part of the map.

Play in the editor, Stop and full screen

Play (or Play in editor) runs the game inside the editor, in the Game view over the 3D view; it needs the DeusEdix app (in a browser, use Play in game window). Click the game to play; Shift+F1 or Release mouse gives the mouse back to the editor.

  • Your game's settings stay yours. Every Play starts the game in a window, on its own copies of the game's settings files (Revision: Revision.ini and RevisionUser.ini; the original game: DeusEx.ini and User.ini), made from yours at each Play and removed after it. A Play never changes your own files: not your full-screen choice, your window size, your key bindings or the map your main menu shows, and the game saves in a save folder kept for the project, not in yours. Settings you change inside the game during a Play are not kept. The copies also turn DirectDraw off, which a game in a window does not need (with it on, the original game waited up to half a minute before its window opened).
  • No white flash. While the game starts, the editor's window stays in front of the other windows, so the game's start-up screens and its window, white for a moment before its first picture, stay behind it: maximize the editor to hide them completely. This happens only when the editor is in front as the game starts, and it ends as soon as you switch to another program (Steam and your browser too) or click the desktop, when you minimize the editor, when the game shows an error box (which then comes to the front), and after 60 seconds at the latest. The game appears in the Game view about a second after its first picture. Play in game window shows the game's own start as it is.
  • Stop (in the Game view's header) closes the game where it is: it asks the game to quit as its own menu's Quit does, so it closes in about a second and nothing is left behind. Only a game that does not close within a few seconds is closed by force. The game never changes your map: your edits stay as they were.
  • Continue in game window moves the game out of the editor into its own window, in front of the others, and you play on there; Stop still closes it. The Game view offers it when it cannot show the game (the game opened full screen, keeps changing its window, or could not be taken in), with Retry embedding where that can help.
  • Full screen works only in the game's own window: press Alt+Enter there (with Play in game window, or after Continue in game window). It lasts for that Play: the next one opens in a window again. Alt+Enter pressed while the game plays inside the editor first moves it into its own window, then switches it to full screen there. Full screen turned on in the game's video settings while it plays inside the editor stops the game with an error: the Game view says so and offers Stop.
  • A mod played from the Mods list (Projects > Mods > Play) also starts on its own copies of the settings, but as you play the game yourself: full screen if you play full screen.
  • If the editor ends unexpectedly while a game plays inside it, the game ends with it; a game in its own window keeps running.

Play preparation: only what changed

Play leaves out what is already done. The compile of the project's package, the build of the map and the install of the packages into the game are each left out when nothing they are made from has changed since they last succeeded, and Messages says so: Package unchanged since it was last compiled: not compiled again., Map unchanged since the last Play: not rebuilt., Packages unchanged since the last Play: not installed again. The preparation of an unchanged map then takes less than half a second.

  • What counts as a change is anything the map's build reads: the map itself (its script or .dx) and its saved changes, prefabs, imported textures, characters, models, animations, sounds and voices, the Story, the project's player character, the packages of other mods the project uses, the project's own packages and the editor itself (after an update of the editor each map is built once more). The map's own script, .dx and saved changes and the player character are compared by their text, so the same text saved again is no change there. Saving another map's changes or installing another map does not count (its Story does), nor do the editor's caches, the catalogs it fills from the game, or the file Steam rewrites at every start.
  • A Rebuild counts as Play's build: after a Rebuild, in any mode, the next Play of the unchanged map does not build it again. A Rebuild in Full, Lighting or AI paths (the arrow beside Rebuild, or the Map menu) is remembered: when the map has to be built again, Play builds it the same way and says so, until you Rebuild in Automatic. A cancelled Rebuild leaves the map as it was (a player character's package compiled before the cancel is kept).

Play scenarios and JC's loadout

As Unity's Play mode scenarios, a play scenario is a way to start Play kept under a name in the project, for every map of it. The list right of Play in the menu bar chooses the one Play starts with: Camera (none: JC below the camera, as the game starts him), one of the scenarios, or Edit scenarios…, which opens the Play window. There Scenario picks the one to change; Save keeps the changes in it, Save as… keeps them under a new name (a name already used is replaced; at most 30 scenarios) and makes it the one Play starts with, Delete deletes it. Play in the window plays the choices as they are, kept or not. A scenario keeps:

  • JC starts: below the camera (where you look when Play starts), at the PlayerStart (or where the mission preset puts him), or at a point kept with the scenario: Here keeps the place below the camera and where it looks. A point belongs to its map: on another map JC starts below the camera.
  • the Mission state and the Other flags, as above;
  • JC's loadout: Items (weapons, ammo and pickups of the game and of your project, each with how many: ammo is an item of its own), Augmentations, Skills (each at a level, Untrained to Master), Skill points, Credits, Health (1 to 100, every part of the body) and a Cheat: God (he takes no damage) or Invisible (enemies do not see him). An empty field is as the game starts him.

The game gives the loadout with its own console commands, before you get the controls. The items lie on the floor in front of JC, not in his inventory (pick them up), at most 40 of them. Where JC starts says how: below the camera or at a point, they are laid in rows across his view, each one taking half a second before you get the controls; at the PlayerStart, the game's own summon puts each one in front of him, all at once with the other commands. The rest goes straight on him. Messages lists every command Play sent (*Loadout: set DeusExPlayer bCheatsEnabled True | augadd AugCloak

set SkillLockpicking CurrentLevel 3set DeusExPlayer Credits 500god*): augadd, summon, god and

invisible 1 work only with the game's cheats on, which the loadout turns on for that game; credits, skill points, health and skill levels are set on JC and his skills.

Checked before Play, as Unity checks a scenario: an item, an augmentation or a skill in neither the game nor your project (a scenario from before a package was taken away), a mission preset that does not end in this map and a point kept on another map are left out. The Play window says so under the loadout, Messages when Play starts, and Play goes on without them; the server checks the classes again.

While the game plays in the editor, its header has Pause (the game's own pause), Slow motion (slomo 0.25; pressed again, slomo 1), Ghost (JC flies through walls; pressed again, walk) and God. Each sends the game's console command through the Console's channel (the Console shows it) and stays pressed while it is on; God starts pressed when the scenario gives it. The buttons wait for the game to run, and while the map is still loading a command waits for it. The game keeps its speed in its settings (slomo saves it there), which during a Play are the Play's own copies: the next game does not start slow.

Known limits of Play

  • After Ctrl+Z of a texture change made over another of your imported textures and already put in by a Rebuild, the next build (Play's or a Rebuild's) can show those objects with their original texture, and the Plays after it keep it: press Rebuild once more.
  • In Revision, a new texture on an object that Revision draws with its own high-definition (HDTP) model (boxes, trash cans, baskets, some lamps and the like) does not show in the game, though the editor shows it.
  • A texture put on All uses in this map (Texture Editor) reaches an object given another model, or one with a model of its own written into a map script since the last build, only from the second build, and the first build may put it on the part the old model wore it on, which then keeps it. Give the object its new model and Rebuild before you put the texture on every object; if a part took it by mistake, take the texture away, Rebuild, then put it on again. On an existing (.dx) map, an object of a pasted prefab with a model of its own is never reached.

Where things are kept

Everything is in DeusEdix's folder (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\DeusEdix), except the maps you install:

tools\mapkit\maps\<map>.edits.jsonyour changes to a map
maps\your maps; Install copies them into the game's Revision\DeusEdixMaps folder
tools\mapkit\story\<map>.jsonthe map's story; UCEd_<map>.uc in src\DeusEdix\Classes is generated from it
tools\mapkit\textures\<map>\the pictures you imported or made in the Texture Editor; textures\_library\ = My textures
tools\mapkit\prefabs\what you copied with Ctrl+C
tools\mapkit\maps\templates\your map templates (File > Save map as template...): a folder each with its edits, picture, the pictures it uses and, for a copied map, that map's file
tools\mapkit\anims\the Animation Studio's animations, skeletons (rigs) and frames (baked); dist\DeusEdixAnims.u is made from them
tools\mapkit\editor\cache\what the editor computed (models, textures, animations of the game, AI paths, class defaults); safe to delete
tools\mapkit\editor\cache\game-revision\, game-vanilla\what is made from the game itself, once for every project of that game (object models and previews, the maps read for the Pieces, texture pictures, conversations, voices, animations, characters): a new project opens with it ready; safe to delete
DevInstall\the workspace made from your game (links to its folders, copies of its settings)
logs\what the program did, for when something goes wrong

When you install a project, DeusEdix puts its maps in a folder of its own inside the game (DeusEdixProjects) and adds it to the game's settings (Revision.ini, or DeusEx.ini for the original game), so the game reads your maps before its own (a backup is kept: Revision.ini.bak_projects). The installer also puts DeusEdixNav.u and DeusEdixNav.dll into each game's System folder: the characters of DeusEdix maps and mods use them to find their way.

To uninstall DeusEdix, use Start > Uninstall DeusEdix, Windows' installed apps, or run the installer again and choose Uninstall. It always takes DeusEdix's lines out of the games' settings and removes the workspace and the caches. It also asks whether to remove everything else DeusEdix put into the games (the maps, saves and mods it installed, and DeusEdixNav, which mods made with DeusEdix need); that is kept unless you tick it. Your projects are never removed: they stay in the Projects folder for a later install.

AI assistant (MCP)

An AI assistant can work in the editor with you: look at the open map, find objects, place and move them, change their properties, write and play-test dialogues, save and rebuild. It goes through MCP (Model Context Protocol), so any assistant that speaks it can connect: Claude Code, or another MCP client.

  1. Tools > AI assistant...: tick Let an AI assistant use this editor. Until you do, no assistant can reach the editor; untick it and none can any more.
  2. Connect the assistant once. The same window shows the command for your PC, for Claude Code: claude mcp add deusedix -- "<DeusEdix>\deusedix-cli.exe" --run "<DeusEdix>\tools\mapkit\deusedix_mcp.py" (the dialog shows your own folders as %LOCALAPPDATA%..., so a screenshot of it does not show your Windows user name; Copy, or copying the text, gives the real command) Other assistants start the same command as an MCP server (stdio).
  3. Open a project and a map in DeusEdix and ask the assistant. The window says when it last called.

What it does happens in the editor as if you clicked: you see it at once, it is unsaved and Ctrl+Z takes it back, until it or you save. It works on the project open in DeusEdix, and only while its page is open. When the editor would ask you to confirm (deleting objects a mission uses), the assistant is told the question and has to ask again saying yes. Its tools:

editor_state, list_maps, open_mapwhat is open, unsaved changes, the selection, the last messages; open a map (with unsaved changes only if told to save or to discard them)
find_objects, get_object, select_objectsthe map's objects by text, class or distance; one object's properties; select them and bring the camera there
find_classes, find_textureswhat can be placed (as the Objects browser lists it), the game's textures by name
place_object, move_object, set_properties, delete_objectsas the Objects browser, the gizmo and the Inspector do; a block (cls "block") and a drawn piece's settings and textures too
drawa wall, road, floor or ceiling through its points, stairs or a roof from A to B, as Map > Draw
batchseveral of these in one call, in order, stopping at the first that fails
undo, redo, save, rebuildas Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y, Save and Rebuild
viewa picture of the 3D view, from where the camera is, from a point towards another, or framing an object; without the gizmo and icons, Lit, Unlit or Shaded
story_list, story_get, story_write, story_playthe map's dialogues, one with its graph, change one or make a new one, Play-test it with flags and answers
run_js, project_apiadvanced: the editor's page and the project's server, for what the other tools do not do

Advanced tools. run_js (JavaScript in the editor's page) and project_api sending changes to the project's server can do whatever the editor can. They work only while Also allow advanced tools is ticked in the same window; otherwise project_api only reads. Keep them off unless you need them: an assistant reads the text of maps, dialogues and mods, and text written by someone else could try to steer it ("prompt injection"). Without them, the worst such text could make it do is an undoable, unsaved change.

What keeps it safe.

  • The editor answers this PC only. It listens on 127.0.0.1, and it refuses any request addressed to another name or sent by another site's page. A web page cannot reach it, even one that points its own name at your PC (DNS rebinding).
  • Every request needs the session of the editor that is running, a new random one each time it starts.
  • The editor's address and session are in assistant.json in your AppData\Local\DeusEdix folder, only while assistants are allowed. Unticking the box deletes them, and the editor checks the box at every call, not only the file.
  • Each tool tells the assistant what it does. One that reads can be let through. One that loses or overwrites something (deleting, saving, Rebuild, discarding changes, rewriting a dialogue) is marked, so an assistant like Claude Code asks you first.
  • While the assistant works, a badge in the corner of the editor says what it is doing.

Local geometry (Experimental)

Enable Settings > Editing > Local geometry (Experimental), then choose Map > Local geometry (Experimental) or the same command in the Rebuild menu. The tool opens a separate window with a private copy of the map currently shown. Rebuild normally first if you want to include saved editor changes; those changes are not applied automatically inside this tool. The temporary tutorial does not use it.

Select a source brush and enter its movement, scale or rotation. These transforms use that brush's pivot. Before / After show the compiled geometry after verification. The default Area · Full check reconstructs the affected area and still uses a Full rebuild to check the result. Isolated box, Convex CSG and Buried detail offer independent verification without Full only for supported geometry. Unsupported changes are refused; a successful edit on one brush does not imply that every edit on the map is supported.

It works the same for Deus Ex: Revision and the original Deus Ex. What the modes without Full accept: Isolated box, a plain box floating inside one room, on a map of at most 24 plain boxes; Convex CSG, a map of at most 24 plain convex brushes, lit or not, with one room and no portals, and an edit whose area leaves out at least one other brush; Buried detail, one detail (semisolid) brush that only grows inside solid. On a lit map the faces a result rebuilt have no light until you rebuild Lighting.

Results are experimental copies outside the normal project build. Show result opens their folder; Continue from result starts another edit from the verified copy. To keep one in your project, use Open map > Browse .dx to import it as a new map. Rebuild its Lighting and Paths before Play or Install. The tool does not replace the project's map or install a result automatically. Verification can take a few minutes and this preview is not a speed guarantee.

The feature uses the engine from your installed game. No game maps, packages, textures or engine DLLs are included in DeusEdix.

Limits

  • Some original maps cannot be rebuilt (Wan Chai Street): only objects can change there.
  • A map's BSP has a limit (65536 nodes): Rebuild optimises it as UnrealEd did, so the big maps of Revision stay under it; a map that still goes over says so in Messages (fewer or simpler blocks there).
  • Stretching a structure of an existing map needs a rebuild of its geometry, so it works only where the map can be rebuilt.
  • The Animation Studio works on human characters (the skeleton has hips, spine, head, arms and legs): animals and robots can play and use the game's animations, not new ones. The fingers and the face do not move (Deus Ex has no bones for them: the mouth is the game's lip sync).
  • New animations are as smooth as the frames of the model allow: a character with few vertices (the original models) bends at the joints like the game's own animations do.
  • Original maps that cannot be rebuilt (Wan Chai Street) cannot get Pieces either (they need a rebuild of the geometry); a copy of such a map has the same limit.
  • Characters of the Creator have no barks (the short lines the game's characters say by themselves) and keep the bodies of the game: new 3D models cannot be imported.
  • The editor does not make voices by itself: record or import them for each line (see Voices).
  • The Lit view is close to the game but not identical: lamps that flicker or pulse are shown steady (a flickering lamp lit, even in a Revision project, whose renderer leaves it dark) and coronas and zone fog are not drawn unless View > Effects is on, and then the walls take an estimate of each lamp's share (their lightmaps hold every lamp together), palette lamps and light effects (torch, searchlight) stay steady, a corona is a glow of its lamp's colour rather than its Skin texture and only walls hide it (in the game objects do too), and volumetric fog is not drawn; some objects can have a different skin in the game.
  • Animations move objects, not characters (their AI walks them: give them orders and patrols). The keys of a door or an elevator are the engine's: at most 8, and the same curve for all of them.
  • Imported models do not move by themselves (no skeletal animations: characters are made with the Character Creator), and their collision is a cylinder round them (untick Solid for a long table JC should reach over). A map that uses them needs DeusEdixModels.u (Install puts it in the game).
  • Hear the map plays each sound as it is: no zone reverb, no walls in between, no Doppler. Robots and fires, whose Properties have no Sound, are heard only if the map gives them one. A zone's Reverb is heard only in the game, with an audio that plays reverb (Galaxy, or Revision's OpenAL audio with its reverb on).
  • A zone works only when its area is closed: walls and zone portals all round. Water and portals change the geometry: they need a map that can be rebuilt.
  • Interactions, stories, cutscenes and the editor's characters run through DeusEdix's package (DeusEdix.u, which Install puts in the game): a map that uses them needs it. Security cameras give no event when they see JC (the game has none): use a zone instead.
  • A door opened by an interaction or a Story action behaves as when JC opens it (it triggers its Event when it has opened): a door that shuts by itself after some seconds (stays open) shuts again.
